# Design logic — what Virt Advisor does, and where Advisor-e ends

> **Purpose.** The guiding framework for this app: the boundary between Virt Advisor and the
> master Advisor-e app, and how the tier logic applies to **every feature built here**.
>
> **Written 2026-08-10** at Mike's request, from his own words that day plus the recorded rulings
> of 2026-07-06, 2026-07-30, 2026-08-09 and 2026-08-10.
>
> **§1 is the most important section in this file.** A first draft of this document explained the
> whole platform's logic — most of which belongs to Advisor-e — and Mike's response was that it
> risked creating *more* confusion, not less. He was right: within two days, two pieces of work
> had been started here that duplicated things Advisor-e already owns. §1 exists to stop the
> third.

---

## 1. 🔴 THE BOUNDARY — read this before designing anything

### 1.1 What belongs to Advisor-e (the master app) — NOT built here, NOT copied here

**Mike, 2026-08-10:**

> *"All of the template cloning, access and editing, hosting and archiving etc. are controlled by
> the Advisor-e app… NONE of this is visible inside the Virt Advisor app since it doesn't need to
> be — NONE of the functionality in this app requires those things."*

| Advisor-e owns | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Login and authentication** | We read a verified JWT. We never issue one |
| **User and account creation** | Every level's accounts are created there |
| **The organisation chart** | Which firms are in which group, which groups in which global group |
| **Roles and permissions** | Including which role value means "group manager" |
| **Templates — the entire lifecycle** | Cloning, access, editing, hosting, archiving. **Each template carries its own id there** for cloning and archiving; we never see it |
| **Tutorial videos** | Same |

**The rule that follows: if a feature needs any of the above, it is not ours.** Do not design a
screen for it, do not hold a copy of its data, do not mint an id for it.

⚠ **Two live examples of getting this wrong, both in August 2026:**

- A **"Global Groups" membership screen** was designed and approved on 2026-08-10, then withdrawn
  within the hour. Reasoning: *"our `firms` table has no group column, so nothing in our data
  says which firms are in which group."* True — and the conclusion was wrong. It only ever meant
  *we don't hold a copy*. The screen would have been a second, drifting copy of Advisor-e's org
  chart.
- **`data/templates.json`** holds 291 records with no id, and this was written up as *"the biggest
  gap in the product."* It is not a gap. Templates are Advisor-e's. The `page`/`link` values on
  those records are **page** ids from the master search-contents script — not template ids, which
  we never receive.

⚠ **The search-contents JSON is generated by the master app. Its ids and content must never be
edited or challenged from here** (CLAUDE.md).

### 1.2 What Virt Advisor is

Three features in one container (Mike, 2026-07-30):

1. **Performance reports** — the Model Library and the report screens
2. **The AI section** — the Virtual Advisor / AI Coach and everything that feeds it
3. **Collaborate** — the Adviser Network

Plus the **Firm Manager Hub**, which is where a manager configures the above for the people
below them.

### 1.3 What this app actually holds

**Not the org chart, not accounts, not templates.** This app holds **advisory decision
configuration**: the material the AI draws on, the decision trees behind it, the language that
marks advisory from compliance, the client journey, and the capability questions — plus the
records of what people did with them.

That is the whole of what cascades here (§3), and the whole of what rolls up (§4).

## 2. Who the user is, and why that shapes every screen

This *is* ours, because it decides our wording, our reports and our tone.

**Mike, 2026-08-10:**

> *"The issue has always been we are most often NOT dealing with skilled, confident and
> experienced advisors. Most are learning; trying to build confidence and acumen."*

Advisor-e delivers **"learning just in time"**. The typical user is learning on the job, in front
of a paying client. The product goal is a **"visible learning culture"** — visible so that people
can be **helped**.

> *"The reports cascade up so we learn what is working, what isn't, who is failing so we can
> offer help… what needs improving, what tools are being missed, how we can make finding the
> right tool for the right client and right advisor easier over time."*

**Consequences that bind on this app's screens:**

- **Tone is help, never score.** This is why the Adoption tab was ruled warmer and advisory
  rather than a league table, and why its quiet-firm threshold is 60 days — a badge means *act*,
  not *glance*.
- **A screen written for a confident specialist is written for the wrong person.**
- **Reports exist to find where the product is failing the learner**, not to rank them.

## 3. What cascades DOWN in this app

### 3.1 The levels, as this app sees them

Advisor-e's hierarchy is `mentor → global_manager → group_manager → firm_manager → advisor →
client`. **We do not implement it — we scope to it.** A global group is a brand (Advisor-e, BDO,
Lindt & Co); a group is normally a country; a firm is a branch.

What "authority flows down" means **here** is narrower than it means in Advisor-e. In this app a
management level does exactly two things to the level below:

1. **Exposure** — what of the level above's configuration is passed on.
2. **Authorship** — editing what it inherited, and adding rows of its own.

**Mike, 2026-08-10:** *"Larger firms and groups want to have the ability to edit the templates
and/or include their own fresh resources — so they can train their people 'the way they like'."*
For templates that happens in Advisor-e (§1.1). **For this app's five configuration blocks below,
it happens here.**

### 3.2 The five blocks that cascade

| Block | What it controls | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain Support** | The material the AI draws on, per advisory area | `deepMerge` |
| **Logic Tables** | The decision trees behind the advice | `deepMerge` |
| **Section placement** | Where items sit on screen, for both of the above | `deepMerge` |
| **Advisory Distinctions** | The phrases marking advisory from compliance | `resolveInheritedRows` |
| **Advisory Staircase** | The client journey steps | `resolveInheritedRows` |
| **Quizzes** | The capability questions | `resolveInheritedRows` |

- **`deepMerge`** — map-shaped config; an untouched entry falls through to the layer above.
- **`resolveInheritedRows`** — each row is a *decision*: switch it off, edit it, reset it, or add
  your own, resolved against the tier above as base.

⚠ **Array-valued keys deliberately cannot cascade.** Arrays replace wholesale, so a level holding
a one-item array would blank the level above's whole set for itself. That is why `templates` and
`logic-lab-accepted` are absent — a correctness decision, not an omission.

### 3.3 Delta, never clone (ruled 2026-08-09)

> *"A level holds only its changes, not a copy."*

An untouched row keeps receiving the level above's improvements automatically. A row a level
**has** edited is protected — the level above's later change is **offered** (*Adopt / Keep mine*),
never silently applied.

**Clone-like protection where someone has made a decision; automatic freshness where they have
not.** A plan built on the word *fold* was put to Mike on 2026-07-30 and he caught the difference.

### 3.4 The advisor is a pass-through *in this app*

Nothing an advisor does here cascades to anyone, and no level inherits from an advisor. Their
final selection and final edit of a **template** happen in Advisor-e (§1.1), not here — so this
app needs no advisor-level override storage. **The client is a recipient only.**

## 4. What rolls UP in this app

### 4.1 The rule

🔴 **EVERY report rolls up. No exceptions.** (Ruled 2026-08-10; already the rule since
2026-07-30.) Each level sees **the level immediately below it, summarised** — not everything
beneath it.

| Level | Sees |
|---|---|
| Firm manager | its advisers |
| Group manager | its firms |
| Global group manager | its groups |
| Mentor | its global groups |

**Never decide report-by-report which tier "should" see something.** That invents per-tier
functionality, contradicts *"every tier is the same screen, re-scoped"*, and creates an exception
list someone must re-derive later.

### 4.2 The reports

| Report | Status |
|---|---|
| **Adviser Network** | ✅ Already rolls up. `GET /api/people/*` resolves the caller's tier from the verified token. The working model for the rest |
| **Team Progress** | Ruled 2026-08-10 |
| **Team Case Studies** | Ruled 2026-08-10. Full case text stays at the firm; each level above sees a summary |
| **How firms are using the app** (Adoption) | Already a roll-up. Needs re-scoping so a middle tier reads only what is beneath it |
| **Case Reviews** | Ruled 2026-08-10 |
| **Logic-Lab Report** | Ruled 2026-08-10. Configuration, never people |
| **Template Check** | Ruled 2026-08-10. The rulings queue, scoped to the level below |

### 4.3 What limits it

- **"The level below" is the limit.** A group manager sees their firms, not a flat roster of every
  adviser in the country.
- **Case sharing stays double opt-in.** The adviser decides whether a case is visible to their
  firm; the firm manager separately decides whether it goes further. The level above sees the
  anonymised copy — never the raw text.
- **The two cross-firm accuracy reads enforce their line in code.** `assertNoPersonalFields`
  **throws** rather than filtering, so the day the shape changes is loud.
- **A roll-up with nothing in it says so on screen.** An empty roll-up and a broken one must never
  look alike.

### 4.4 🔴 The mistake this section exists to prevent

On 2026-08-10 a tab matrix was drawn leaving Team Progress, Team Case Studies and the three
accuracy reports **out** of both middle tiers, reasoning from a 2026-08-09 ruling that kept Team
Progress away from the mentor.

**That ruling was about an OUTSIDE party** — Advisor-e — seeing a customer's staff. A global group
is a **brand**, so a global or group manager is the **customer's own senior person** looking at
their **own** firms. Applying an external-party privacy boundary to internal managers inverts it.
Nothing in the code could have noticed, and no test could have failed.

## 5. Feature by feature — how the tiers apply

### 5.1 The Firm Manager Hub — one screen, re-scoped

**Ruled 2026-07-30:** *"every tier is the same screen, re-scoped… there's no new functionality."*

[`pages/mentor.vue`](../pages/mentor.vue) and [`pages/firm-manager.vue`](../pages/firm-manager.vue)
both render the **same** [`components/FirmManagerHub.vue`](../components/FirmManagerHub.vue) with a
different `scope` prop. The two middle tiers need thin pages and correct scoping — not new
features.

Its tabs, and what each is:

| Tab | Kind | Gate today |
|---|---|---|
| Domain Support | cascading config | all scopes |
| Logic Tables | cascading config | all scopes |
| Logic-Lab | firm-local record | all scopes |
| Advisory Staircase | cascading config | all scopes |
| Advisory Distinctions | cascading config | **two separate items**: `scope === 'firm'` and `scope === 'mentor'` |
| Quizzes | cascading config | all scopes |
| Adviser Network | roll-up (Collaborate) | all scopes |
| Team Progress | roll-up | `scope !== 'mentor'` |
| Adoption | roll-up | `scope === 'mentor'` |
| Team Case Studies | roll-up | `scope !== 'mentor'` |
| Logic-Lab Report | roll-up | `scope === 'mentor'` |
| Template Check | roll-up | `scope === 'mentor'` |
| Case Reviews | roll-up | `scope === 'mentor'` |
| Templates & Videos | — | **dormant, `v-if="false"`** — templates are Advisor-e's (§1.1) |

🔴 **The tab-gate trap, and it is why tabs must name their tiers.** The gates above are written as
negatives and exclusive pairs, not as tier lists. The moment a third scope value exists:

- **Team Progress and Team Case Studies switch themselves ON** at the new tiers (`!== 'mentor'` is
  true for them), and
- **Advisory Distinctions disappears entirely** — neither of its two gates matches a third scope.

**Nothing errors and no test fails**, because no test asserts what a scope that does not yet exist
should show. Rewriting these gates to name their tiers is part of building the middle tiers, not a
follow-up.

### 5.2 The AI section

Domain Support and Logic Tables are the two blocks that feed it, and both cascade (§3.2). The
material a firm's AI draws on is therefore the mentor's, plus every edit made at each level in
between, plus the firm's own — resolved per field.

**All AI logic and the OpenAI key are backend-only** (Stack Constitution req. 7). Nothing about
the tiers changes that.

### 5.3 Performance reports

The Model Library and the report screens (`business-performance-report`, `debtor-drag`,
`margin-breakeven`, `ebitda-dcf`, `quick-position`, `lease-vs-buy`, `loan-estimator`,
`eight-levers`, `cost-of-capital`) are **client-facing calculators run by an advisor**. They do
not cascade and do not roll up — they are per-client work product, and their governance is the
report visual standard, not the tier chain.

What *does* roll up is the **record of use** — which is Team Progress and Adoption (§4.2).

### 5.4 Collaborate / Adviser Network

**Already tier-aware, and the working model for everything else.** One component renders the
console for every manager tier; the backend returns the correctly-scoped payload; scope is
re-derived server-side on every request and never trusted from the client.

### 5.5 Advisor Progress and the CPD record

Per-advisor records. They roll up as Team Progress (§4.2). The CPD export is the advisor's own,
sent to their accounting society.

## 6. Scope identity — how a tier is named in our storage

One seam: [`server/utils/tierChain.js`](../server/utils/tierChain.js) — `parentScopeOf` /
`scopeChain`. Reserved scope ids ride the existing `firm_id` column per the reserved-row ruling of
2026-08-09:

| Tier | Scope id | Row in `firms` |
|---|---|---|
| Mentor | `__platform__` | seeded |
| Global manager | `__global__:<brand>` | one per global group |
| Group manager | `__group__:<brand>:<country>` | one per country |
| Firm manager | the real firm id | already there |

Double underscores make a collision with a real Advisor-e firm id impossible.

⚠ **NOT the `group` table.** `config/db-schema.sql` has one — it is a Collaborate **Special
Interest Group** (social), not a management tier. Reading it as one would be a correctness bug.

**Own-row id prefixes must stay distinct per tier**, or one level switching off "its own" row
silently drops another's: mentor `ms-`/`mq-`, global `xs-`/`xq-`, group `gs-`/`gq-`, firm
`fs-`/`fq-`. (`x` for global because `g` reads as *group*.) A test fails if anyone reuses a letter.

🔴 **The reserved-row trap.** A tier's save is **foreign-key refused while the dev fallback reports
success** — the trap that ran the mentor's own saves silently broken for weeks. Every new tier
scope needs its reserved row in `firms` seeded, or its saves vanish while the screen says they
worked. Written down three times now.

## 7. What is not working yet, and whose it is

| Gap | Whose |
|---|---|
| **No middle-tier login exists.** `roles.js` maps only `platform_admin` → mentor and `firm_manager` → firm_manager. No role value produces `global_manager` or `group_manager`. ⚠ `mentor` was never added either | **Advisor-e** |
| **No membership data.** The `firms` table has no country, group or parent column, so `parentScopeOf` returns the mentor scope for every firm and the chain runs mentor → firm, exactly as before | **Advisor-e** |
| **The two middle-tier hub pages** are designed and approved but not built | Ours |
| **The tab gates** (§5.1) must be rewritten to name their tiers — **first**, not last | Ours |
| **The coaching reference does not inherit.** Its 15 rows carry stable `cr-` ids; it simply never joined `resolveInheritedRows`, and its firm side is append-only | Ours |
| **Logic-Lab does not cascade** — firm-local by nature, array-shaped accepted list | Ours (by design) |

⚠ **The middle tiers cannot be demonstrated by logging in as one**, because no such login exists.
They are evidenced by tests against a seeded membership map — a weaker claim than a live screen,
and recorded as one rather than glossed.

## 8. Binding design rules

1. **If a feature needs login, accounts, the org chart, roles or templates, it is Advisor-e's**
   (§1.1). Do not build it here, do not hold a copy of its data, do not mint an id for it.
2. **This repo never invents a role-value name.** The vocabulary is fixed and shared: `mentor,
   global_manager, group_manager, firm_manager, advisor, client`.
3. **No new code takes a bare `firmId` as its scope.** A route resolves a tier and a scope; a firm
   is one possible value, never the assumed one.
4. **Resolution walks the chain** — platform → global group → group → firm — and a level with no
   override is simply absent, not an error.
5. **A level holds only its changes, not a copy** (§3.3). Untouched rows stay current; edited rows
   are protected and the update is *offered*.
6. **Every tab and every report names the tiers it belongs to.** Never gate on a negative — the
   day a new tier exists, a negative gate answers for it silently (§5.1).
7. **Every report rolls up, to the level immediately below, summarised** (§4.1). No per-report
   exceptions.
8. **A tier with nothing to show says so on screen.** An empty roll-up and a broken one must never
   look alike.
9. **Every new tier scope needs its reserved row in `firms`**, or its saves are FK-refused while
   the screen reports success (§6).
10. **Tone is help, not score** (§2). The users are learning.
