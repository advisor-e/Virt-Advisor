# Primary Issue Map — All 14 Advisory Domains

**Status:** Workshop 1 complete — 2026-06-02
**Author:** Mike Barnes (domain expert)
**Purpose:** Foundation for CaseState Layer 2 derivation design. This is the canonical list of primary business situations an advisor diagnoses, across all 14 domains. Feeds Workshop 2 (Solution Categories) and Workshop 3 (Signal Roles).

---

## Domain 1: Profitability & Feasibility

**Profitability:**
- Cost of sales has increased (materials, labour, supplier costs eating into margin)
- Excessive discounting eroding margin (pricing confidence or competitive pressure)
- Low sales volume pulling profit down (revenue is the constraint, not cost)
- Fixed overhead costs grown beyond what revenue can support

**Feasibility:**
- Asset utilisation below viability threshold (the numbers don't stack up)
- Finance strain — funding structure or repayment creating pressure
- Asset realisation or venture extraction — owner needs to understand what they can get out or how to exit the risk

---

## Domain 2: Staff

- Too few qualified staff — insufficient headcount to meet demand
- Inexperienced or insufficiently trained staff — capability gap in the existing team
- No internal training structures — no system for building capability over time
- Poor management practices — weak coaching and accountability from leadership
- Poor communication and feedback standards — team isn't getting the information or direction it needs
- Roles and responsibilities poorly defined — people don't know what they own
- Weak hiring practices — no structured process for bringing the right people in

---

## Domain 3: Data

- No enforceable data capture methods — data either isn't being collected or there's no system to ensure it is
- Poor data integrity — over-reliance on manual input creating errors and unreliable numbers
- Lack of financial controls — no transparency or governance over purchase and sale decisions
- Too much lag indicator data, not enough lead indicators — the business is always looking backwards, never forward
- Narrow data spread — financial performance tracked but business activities, operational metrics, or inventory effectiveness ignored or underreported

---

## Domain 4: Sales & Marketing

**Sales:**
- Low sales volume — not enough revenue coming through the door
- Poor product fit or market acceptance — what's being sold isn't landing with buyers
- Product or service uncompetitive or outdated — the market has moved on
- Supply line disruptions or poor quality controls — delivery problems undermining sales
- No visible sales process — no structured way of moving prospects to clients
- Poor sales training — the team doesn't know how to sell effectively

**Marketing:**
- Poor outbound messaging and lack of marketing systems — nothing going out consistently
- No defined target market personas — the business doesn't know who it's selling to
- Poor positioning or brand perception — how the business is seen doesn't match what it wants to be known for
- No defined marketing statements — the business can't answer: what do we say, to whom, and when

---

## Domain 5: Financial Management

- Poor financial literacy — owner focused on wrong numbers (chasing sales volume while ignoring margin or fixed costs)
- Artisan-over-commercial mindset — business decisions driven by craft or quality pride rather than market demand; "build the best widget" while the market wants good enough at a fair price
- Over-trading — growth funded primarily through debt, leaving the business dangerously exposed to economic downturns or competitive pressure
- Cost structure imbalance — decisions based on assumptions rather than data, resulting in a cost base that doesn't reflect reality

---

## Domain 6: Governance & Leadership

- Poor boardroom dynamics or partner/owner disputes — conflict or misalignment at the top affecting the business
- Poor decision quality — no structured process for making and committing to decisions
- Weak communication of expectations with no documentation — people aren't held accountable for actions or inaction because nothing is written down, and certain personality types avoid confrontation at all costs
- Culture left to chance — no deliberate effort to define and shape what the business stands for
- Personality and skill diversity not actively pursued — team not built for complementary strengths; ideology substituted for performance-driven thinking

---

## Domain 7: Strategy & Planning

- Lack of clarity or belief that the current business model will remain competitive — owner isn't sure the business has a viable future in its current form
- Poor business metrics or undefined operational objectives — no clear targets means inefficient or ineffective activities continue unchallenged
- No defined objectives means no communicated direction — leads to low staff engagement or staff anxiety about job security

---

## Domain 8: Systems

- Processes are either undefined or over-engineered — both result in tasks being done ineffectively or inconsistently
- No regular structured review of practices — the business never stops to ask whether what it's doing is working or could be improved
- Siloed operations — departments or people working independently with no coordination, creating gaps, duplication, and miscommunication

---

## Domain 9: Valuation

- Inconsistent financial performance reducing the capitalised future earnings component — unreliable earnings history lowers what a buyer will pay
- Asset values unsubstantiated — assets on the books aren't supported by current independent evidence
- Stock and work in progress figures inflated — inventory or WIP carried at values that don't reflect reality
- Goodwill calculations driven by market growth assumptions rather than the debt-servicing capacity of the business — valuation built on what the market might do, not what a buyer can actually finance

---

## Domain 10: Risk Management

- No process to identify and mitigate risks — the business has no systematic way of seeing risks before they become problems
- Over-reliance on insurance — default response to risk is to insure it rather than actively avoid or reduce exposure
- Poorly defined risk assessment — no framework for understanding probability of a risk occurring versus the financial consequence if it does

---

## Domain 11: Succession Planning

- Owner has no defined life after work — fear of losing purpose or status causes them to undermine or delay succession activities
- Business scale or profitability insufficient to support both withdrawing owners and incoming successors — the numbers don't work for a clean transition
- Sibling or family inequality — emotions and family dynamics driving role appointments and business decisions instead of capability or business need
- No clear succession pathway — a general idea exists but no executable plan with defined steps, timelines, or accountabilities

---

## Domain 12: Conflict Meetings

- Accumulated tolerance — partners who have avoided difficult conversations for too long eventually reach a breaking point, with resentment built up over past events
- Ego, personality, and ethics clash — differences in values, working style, and personal fears combine to create a toxic dynamic between partners
- Adversity exposes incompatibility — pressure and hardship reveal how differently partners cope, and what each sees as the other's greatest weakness surfaces at the worst possible moment

---

## Domain 13: End of Year Meetings

- Clients treat advice as worthless — heard but not acted on; the meeting is a compliance formality
- Accountants mistake compliance comprehension for client value — assuming that because the client understands their tax position, the meeting was meaningful
- Knowledge gap blocks the upsell — the advisor wants to offer value-added services but the client lacks foundational understanding to recognise what they need or why they'd pay for it; you can't ask an uneducated client to make an educated purchase decision

---

## Domain 14: Due Diligence

- A transaction is in progress — the seller must prove the business is as good as represented, while the buyer's job is to find everything that was too good to be true before the deal closes

---

## Design Notes

- Domain 1 (Profitability) and Domain 4 (Sales & Marketing) share "low sales volume" — the same signal produces a primary issue in two domains depending on advisor focus. CaseState must handle cross-domain signals.
- Domain 14 (Due Diligence) has one core situation with two sides (seller vs buyer), not multiple distinct situations.
