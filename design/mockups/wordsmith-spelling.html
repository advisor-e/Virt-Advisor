Wordsmith: English spelling

Item 13.7. Mike's ruling, 2026-10-02: each firm chooses whether Wordsmith spells English the New Zealand way or the US way, starting as New Zealand. It only matters when the client spoke English. In any other language Wordsmith writes in that language and checks no spelling.

Approved by Mike, 2026-10-02, with one change he approved the same day: the two sentences that said "your firm" now read correctly at every level ("Your own choice."; "...so you still use {yours}.").

The outlined block is the only new thing on the screen. Everything around it is the Wordsmith tab as it is today.

Who sets it

The same as every other Wordsmith setting. The mentor sets the starting value, and each level below may keep it or choose for itself. A choice passes down until someone below changes it.

Mentor: New Zealand→ Global group manager→ Group manager→ Firm manager

Where it goes: the top of Style wording

Spelling applies to all five statements, so it sits under the Style wording chip, above the sentence length, formality, jargon and voice wording, rather than inside one statement.

What changes behind the screen

The spelling check follows the choice

  • New Zealand: as today, a US spelling such as "color" is flagged and the draft is retried once.
  • US: the same list read the other way round, so "colour" is flagged instead.
  • Any other language: no spelling check, as built today.

What stays fixed

  • Two choices only, New Zealand and US. Adding a third is a new decision.
  • The language itself always follows what the client spoke. This setting never makes Wordsmith write English to a client who spoke German.
  • A rewrite uses the spelling in force when it runs.
