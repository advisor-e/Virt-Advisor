A scanned page — a photograph of a slide rather than a slide — has no text and no shapes to convert, so it would come through as an unreadable block. 7 · Six questions, one recommendation each

1. The frame — how does an imported page stop looking foreign?

🔴 RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-23, AS RECOMMENDED. The app's frame is painted over the outer edge of the imported page, covering the border the source deck carried. Every concept in a session wears one frame and it is the app's.

Your deck pages carry their own cyan border. The app's 35 drawn slides carry the five blue bars, pinned identically by test. Both cannot show at once.

Recommended: the app's frame is painted over the outer edge of the imported page, covering whatever border the source carried, so every concept in a session wears one frame and it is ours.

Against: it paints over a thin strip of the original, so a page with content hard against its edge would lose a sliver. Measured on your Organisational Review page, the margin is comfortably clear — but that is one page, and you have ruled the converter is not to be run across your other decks, so this is checked per import at the preview rather than assumed.

Rejected: cropping the source border automatically. It means guessing where somebody else's border ends, and it guesses wrong silently.

2. One Add Concept button, or one per section?

🔴 RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-23, AS RECOMMENDED — "one". A single Add Concept button, with the section chosen as the first field, and pre-filled wherever the screen already knows which section you are in. Four per-section buttons are not built.

You offered both: "Each section … has a 'Add Concept' button … or — one place with a droptab asking which section to be posted into."

Recommended: one button, with the section chosen as the first field, as drawn at §3.

Against: one more click than a button that already knows the section. Four buttons take four times the space for one action, and the section is a field the app already holds, so it can be pre-filled wherever the screen already knows which section you are in.

3. Is the Response Form required, or optional?

🔴 RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-23, AGAINST THE RECOMMENDATION, AND HE IS RIGHT. In his words: "Every concept has a teach and response requirement. what you are seeing is some concepts require more than a single page of teach material."

The Response Form is REQUIRED, and the teaching half accepts more than one page. The "This concept teaches only — skip" control is not built.

⚠ The recommendation rested on a figure that measured the wrong thing, recorded here so nobody re-derives it. It said "24 of your 46 existing concepts have no response table". That count is of concepts whose response form has not been chosen yet — which is what items 15.11 and 15.16 are already on the list for — not of concepts that need none. The data agrees with Mike: responsePage already names a later page on five concepts — Vertical Integration teaches on p22 and responds on p24, Revenue Streams p33 → p34, The 8 Profit Levers p39 → p41 — and two of them share one response page.

4. Is the source PDF kept after conversion?

🔴 RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-23, AS RECOMMENDED. The original PDF is kept, stored against the version of the concept it produced.

⚠ The reason is load-bearing, not housekeeping. The conversion library's own authors warn the part we use "is no longer maintained and may be removed in the future". Keeping the originals is what makes a future change of converter possible without asking every firm to upload its material again — and without them, concepts imported today would be frozen as they are for good.

Recommended: kept, against the version of the concept it produced.

Against: it stores more of a firm's material than the app strictly needs. For it: a concept can be re-made if the conversion improves, and anyone can check the slide against the page it came from — which is the same reason the 35 drawings are generated from their artefacts rather than written by hand.

5. Does a concept added above appear below at once, or does the level below opt in?

🔴 RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-23, AS RECOMMENDED. A concept added at one level appears at once at every level below, unless that level has replaced it. There is no opt-in, and no second cascade pattern is built.

Recommended: it appears at once, inherited, exactly as the approved Depreciation Rates cascade already works — the nearest level above is what you get until you replace it.

Against: a firm sees new material without asking for it. For it: a second pattern for the same idea is how two things that should agree drift apart.

6. What does "client / business entity" mean as a level?

🔴 RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-23, AND IT IS NOT WHAT THIS DRAWING FIRST RECOMMENDED. In his words: "where it ends up - the way the rest of the feature currently works - delivering a pdf."

The client/business entity is the DESTINATION, not an authoring level. Concepts are authored at the four manager levels and delivered to the client in the session and in the plan PDF, exactly as everything else already reaches them. Nothing is authored there, and no new storage or scope is built.

⚠ The recommendation this replaced was the largest single piece of the item, and it was wrong. It read the phrase as a fifth place where a concept could be created — "a concept added at this level belongs to that one client's plan and appears nowhere else" — and would have built a new scope and new storage for it. The ruling removes that work entirely. It also means TIERS in server/utils/tierChain.js needs no change: mentor → global group manager → group manager → firm manager is the whole cascade, and the fifth step was never a tier.
