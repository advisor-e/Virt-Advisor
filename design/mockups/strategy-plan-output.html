1. The assembly rule, read off the output itself

Pivot has a rhythm, and it is not the one the September drawing assumed. It does not alternate teach-then-capture one concept at a time. It teaches a whole step, then captures the whole step — and it announces that step twice, once for discussion and once for action.

The five rules, each provable on a numbered page

1 · Teaching and capture interleave per STEP, never per concept.

Teach the whole step, then capture the whole step. Pages 4–12 are one step done twice over.

2 · A concept can be captured more than once, into the same table.

Porter's takes observations on page 11 — "record your observations ONLY. (For Now)" — and responses on page 21, in a different step. A model in which a concept appears once in a session cannot produce Pivot. The capture record keys on the visit, not on the concept.

3 · A concept can bring more than one teaching slide.

Progression of Economic Value brings two — pages 15 and 16, the ladder and then Pine's output/imperative/sensitivity table.

4 · Capture slides come in two kinds.

The table drawn on the page (22, 23, 28, 30), and a pointer to a separate table (11, 12, 21, 24, 26). Both are in the document; only the second sends the client to a workbook.

5 · The agenda is authored, not derived from the ticks.

Step 5, "Do It & Review It", is on page 3's agenda and has no slides at all. The discussion/action split is a choice too — step 3 does without it. Nothing here can be computed from the tick list alone.

Market Diffusion Theory

Market Diffusion Theory relates to the concept of market Acceptance and Saturation. The theory explores the natural stages required to bring a new product (or service) to market; the costs and time associated and the likely timing of increased competition once the market is 'educated'.

The Key difference between market diffusion and the product life cycle is that (with diffusion theory) a market may have reached saturation whilst the product still enjoys acceptance. Whereas (with product life cycle) it's the product itself that is considered obsolete.

4. The decisions — all 5 settled

Each is one recommendation and one question a yes or no answers. Asked one at a time in chat; recorded here so the ruling and what was proposed both survive — a later session can see which way a decision went and whether it went against the recommendation.

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-17. One continuous document of slide-shaped pages. It scrolls as one document on screen and is his deck page for page when printed or presented. 🔴 THE CONSEQUENCE IS BINDING: there is ONE artefact, never two formats. A build that produces a web report and a separate exported deck has created two things that can disagree — and the page is a 16:9 frame from the start, not a reflowing column that is later squeezed into one.

His words pull slightly apart: "1 seamless document" and "AS IT CURRENTLY APPEARS in the slides". A document scrolls; a slide is a fixed 16:9 frame.

Recommendation: one continuous document of slide-shaped pages, as drawn in §3. On screen it scrolls as one thing; printed or presented it is your deck, page for page. Nothing is lost and it needs no second format.

What this rejects, recorded so it is not re-proposed: a free-flowing web report. It would read better on a phone and could never be put on a projector in a room, which is where a planning session is actually run.

Asked as: is the output a continuous document of slide-shaped pages?
