  2. ✓ How a sale is entered — RULED 2026-09-03, and drawn above. Mike: “i would prefer to list the asset, (what) and price etc but then tick sell or purchase next to them, instead of entering negative financial figures”. So every amount is positive and a Buy / Sell tick carries the direction. The reason it is the safer shape, not merely the nicer one: a minus sign puts the direction inside the value, so −12,000 ticked as a sale has two possible readings and the screen has to guess. With the tick there is nothing to guess — money cannot silently flow the wrong way in the cash forecast, and a negative figure is now always a mistake, so it can simply be refused. It also makes the two totals sums of a flag rather than of a sign. One deviation from your words: you said the tick sits next to the asset; it is drawn one column further along, beside the Amount, so the direction is read at the moment the figure is typed rather than three columns earlier. One line to move back if you would rather it sat where you said.
  3. ✓ What the sale figure means — RULED 2026-09-03: it is the SALE PRICE, and the screen says what that costs. Your workbook takes the sale figure off the asset's book value and banks it as the money received, so the two agree only when an asset sells for exactly its written-down value. Sell a van worth 8,000 for 12,000 and it books 12,000 in and 12,000 off the books — no gain on sale appears anywhere. That is your model's own method and it is unchanged. The column is headed Price and one line sits under the list: “The forecast assumes an asset sells for its book value, so a gain or loss on sale is not shown.” Chosen because sale price is the figure an advisor actually has — nobody knows a written-down value without looking it up, and a field that cannot be filled accurately gets filled inaccurately — and because the cash line is what a three-way forecast exists for. The cost is confined to depreciation on an asset already sold; it never touches cash. The accountant-correct alternative was two fields, proceeds and book value, and it was rejected as too large a change to your model rather than as wrong: the P&L's two Other Income lines are ANNUAL and spread evenly over twelve months, so a June gain would smear across the year. It would need a new month-by-month P&L row, which flows into retained earnings and changes the shape of the 10,155-cell golden set — a tenth deviation, and structurally larger than any of the nine, which were all repairs to arithmetic already written. Available as its own decision later if you want it. One word taken from your own sentence: the column is headed Price because you wrote “list the asset, (what) and price etc”.
