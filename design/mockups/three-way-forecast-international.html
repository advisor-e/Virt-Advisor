WHY this is the section a bank reads first

Today all of this would appear as one line called paid to creditors, in the month the supplier was settled. The deposit four months earlier, the freight, the duty and the GST at the border would all be invisible — and they are the reason the business needs the money.

Two of these five rows come back: the border GST is reclaimed on the next return, and the stock eventually sells. A funding request is about the months in between, and that is exactly what these rows draw.

What changes in the GST return

Three corrections, all of them cash

1. Overseas sales stop being charged GST. Today every sale is charged, so an exporter's return is overstated every filing period. With the tick set, no GST arises on them at all.

2. GST on imported stock moves to the border. Today it would fall when the supplier is paid. It really falls when the goods clear customs, and it is charged on the landed value — stock plus freight plus duty — not on the supplier's invoice alone.

3. It is still claimed back. Border GST is an input credit on the same return as everything else, so the forecast shows the payment and the credit in their real months rather than netting them into one figure.

There is also one piece of plumbing with no decision in it: on the Cash GST basis the return is currently worked backwards from total money received. Once some receipts are zero-rated that sum is wrong, so the two streams' collections have to be counted separately.
