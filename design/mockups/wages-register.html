🔴 Three faults in the source workbook — sheet 6, DD Considerations

They all sit in one column, H, "Annual Leave Liability" — and that column is what the whole 63,154 headline is summed from (L54:L56, three sumifs over H8:H39).

1 · The one surviving formula points at an empty cell. H8 is (E8*$D$5)*G8. D5 is blank — the 8-hour figure it means to use is in E4, one cell away. As written the formula evaluates to 0; the 1,915.20 on screen is a stale cached value from before the reference broke.

2 · Every other row in the column is a hand-typed number, not a formula. H9 through H39 are constants. Change someone's pay rate or their leave and the liability does not move.

3 · And this is the one that matters — 24 of the 29 people are priced from the wrong column. Only five rows carry an accrued-leave figure at all (G9, G10, G21, G32, G38). On the other twenty-four the typed liability is pay rate × 8 × sick leave consumed — column F. Mary G is the clearest case: no accrued leave recorded, 12 days of sick leave consumed, and a liability of 1,915.20, which is 19.95 × 8 × 12. Where a row has both, the accrued column wins (Agatha: 14 sick, 13 accrued, liability 1,976 = 19 × 8 × 13) — so the intent is not in doubt, only the execution.

Sick leave consumed is a cost already spent. Accrued annual leave is money the business still owes. They are not the same quantity and one is not an estimate of the other. A liability an acquirer prices the business on is the last figure that should be carrying it.

What the port does: computes the liability from accrued annual leave only, every row, always — and where no accrued figure has been entered it says “not yet priced” rather than reaching for a number that is to hand. It does not reproduce the workbook's 63,154, and it should not: on the sample data the honest figure is 10,672 across the five people who have a leave balance recorded. The golden test pins the difference and says why.

🔴 And a wrong count on the drawing you have already seen

wages-model.html's leave-liability card reads Vital 9 · Beneficial 10 · Dispensable 9, total 28. The workbook says 10 · 10 · 9, total 29 (L50:L52, three countifs).

The card's own arithmetic proves which is right. It gives the Vital band an average of 3.7 years, and the workbook's total years for that band is 37 (M58). 37 ÷ 10 = 3.7. Had there been 9 people it would read 4.1. Step 1's "29 sample rows" is the same 29.

Corrected on that drawing in the same change as this one, along with its two notes that were overtaken by your own later rulings — the Revenue at risk / Vital / Beneficial / Dispensable table, which Decision 7 replaced, and the note saying the register is deleted at deal-end, which Decision 8 reversed.

The four open questions

Question 1 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-15 — the register carries its own field

Ruled as recommended: a single typed field on the register, "Hours in a day's leave", starting empty and applying to everyone. Until it is set nothing is priced — a firm that never sets it gets no figure, rather than a figure resting on an assumption of ours. It is drawn above the table, because a setting that changes every number on a sheet belongs on the face of it, not in a menu.

Recommended: a single typed field on the register, "Hours in a day's leave", starting empty, applying to everyone. The liability stays blank until it is set.

Why not take it from step 2. Step 2 already asks for "Average Working Hrs per Day (incl Travel)", and it is tempting to reuse it. But that figure is how long a working day actually runs, travel included — it moves when the work changes. A leave day is contractual. Tying the two would make the money a business owes its staff move every time someone's travel time is re-estimated.

Why not simply use 8. It is the workbook's figure (E4, typed) and it is a fair default — but it is typed because it varies, and hard-coding it is exactly the fault this model has already been corrected for once: "every typed cell reachable, nothing quietly fixed as a constant".

Question 2 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-15 — the gap shows on the document

Ruled as recommended, and it is what is drawn above: an unpriced row reads "not yet priced", and the totals carry a Priced count beside the People count — 5 of 29. A partial total never presents itself as a whole one, and the count doubles as the advisor's list of whose figures to go and get.

Recommended, and drawn above: the row reads "not yet priced", and the totals carry a Priced count beside the People count — 5 of 29 — so the gap is on the face of the document.

Why not just total the ones you have. That is what the workbook effectively does, and it produces a single confident number with no hint that five-sixths of the workforce is missing from it. An acquirer reading 10,672 as the leave liability of a 29-person business would be misled by a figure that is technically correct.

Question 3 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-15 — sick leave comes off

Ruled as recommended, in his words: "take it off". Sick leave consumed is not carried at all — not on the screen, not in the stored record, not read from the payroll import when that is built. The workbook's column F is left where it is; nothing in the product reads it.

Recommended: no. It is not on the screen drawn above.

Two reasons, and either alone would do. Once the liability is computed from the right column, nothing reads it — and this model's own rule is that a typed cell nothing reads earns no control. And it is the most sensitive field on the sheet: how much sick leave a named person has taken is medical-adjacent data about someone who consented to nothing, on a document being prepared for an acquirer. The same reasoning that kept date of birth off the register applies here with more force.

If you want it, it is a column and a stored field — but it should be a decision taken out loud, which is why it is a question rather than an omission.

Question 4 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-15 — its own dial, the same mechanism

Ruled as recommended: the staff register gets its own firm-level retention dial, stored the way the meeting one is but under its own key, set in Firm Manager beside it. The register shows only the resulting date; changing it is a Firm Manager decision. The recommendation was corrected before it was put — see the warning below.

Recommended: the same mechanism, its own setting — one firm-level dial for staff registers, stored exactly as the meeting one is (firmOverlay, cascading the tier chain, version history free) but under its own key, set in Firm Manager beside it. The register shows the resulting date in plain words, as drawn: "kept until 15 September 2033".

⚠ This corrects what this page said first, which was "the firm's existing retention dial" — reading server/utils/meetingRetention.js showed there is no general one. What exists is meeting-retention, and its own file says it is how long a firm keeps a meeting transcript. Reusing that number would tie two unlike things together: the transcript period is spoken aloud to a client in the recorded consent wording, so changing what a firm promises a client would silently change how long it keeps registers of named staff, and the reverse. One dial, two promises, is the kind of coupling nobody remembers at the moment they move it.

Why not a dial per register. Decision 8 settled that the register is kept rather than deleted at deal-end, and gave the reason: a restructure is challenged years later, and both sides need the record. A per-register dial would let one register be set to a shorter period than the firm's own policy, which is the setting most likely to be regretted and the least likely to be reviewed.

What this screen adds is the sentence, not the setting. The date is shown here because a document holding named people should say on its face how long it lives; changing it is a Firm Manager decision, and the button goes there.
