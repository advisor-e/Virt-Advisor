What this says

On these figures the family cannot buy all five properties with $315,000. The first property absorbs the whole deposit and the other four are shown borrowing 100% of their purchase price — the rentals together stand at 90.9% loan to value, which no ordinary investment lender will advance.

The portfolio costs them $4,636 a week in year 1 and stays cash-negative for all ten years, easing to about $1,433 a week by year 10. Across the ten years it asks them for $1,566,523 of cash on top of the deposit.

Against that it builds $2,972,139 of equity by year 10 on $1,881,523 of money put in — a return of 58.0%. The question this screen puts to the client is not whether the portfolio grows. It is whether they can carry $4,678 a week for the first five years while it does.

THE SHAPE, AND THE ALTERNATIVE THAT WAS NOT DRAWN — Q11.

Five properties carry about twenty-five inputs each and four ten-year tables each. Drawn flat that is a screen with a hundred and twenty-five input boxes and twenty tables, and no advisor would find anything on it.

What is drawn: the portfolio is the screen, and one property is open inside it. The left column holds the household, the list of five, the tax rules, and the open property's own figures. The right column holds the portfolio — findings, deposit table, lending, the five compared, the consolidated report, servicing, the commentary — and then the open property's tables at the foot. Opening a different property changes only the two property cards on the left and the section at the foot. Nothing in the portfolio moves.

The alternative, turned down but recorded so it is not re-derived: a portfolio screen that links out to five separate property screens, each one Phase 1 as it stands today. It is less to build and each screen stays simple. It was turned down because the deposit is a portfolio-wide decision made property by property — changing property 3's deposit changes what properties 4 and 5 can borrow — and splitting that across two screens means the advisor never sees the consequence of the number they are typing. If you prefer it, say so and the drawing changes; that is a one-line instruction and it is why this paragraph exists.

WHAT THIS DRAWING DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO.
