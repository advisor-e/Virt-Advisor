The decisions

Settled by the original — no decision needed

Where the mark goes (footer left, cover top right), what size (92×26 and 180×56), the footer line, the page number and the typography are all copied from the approved report drawing. Nothing here is a new choice, which is the point of copying it.

Also settled — A4 versus 16:9 is not a real difference

The original page is 16/9 on screen and drops the ratio in @media print (height:100vh), so it fills an A4 landscape sheet. The plan is 297/210 on Mike's 2026-09-21 ruling. On paper the two are identical — both are A4 landscape. Only the screen preview differs, and each keeps the ruling made for it.

Decision A — how far the firm's colour goes

Mike, 2026-09-22, asked for the firm's colours as well as the logo: “make it complete and get it all sorted once and for all.” The original's palette is Advisor-e's — navy, blue, cyan — which sits awkwardly against his 2026-09-18 ruling that a client's document always shows the advisor's brand and never Advisor-e's.

Recommended: the firm's colour replaces the house palette on the cover and the step dividers — the solid-colour sheets, where a brand is actually seen — and nowhere else. Structural colours stay: navy ink, grey rules, and above all the green/amber/red signal colours, because those mean good, caution and danger, and recolouring them would make the document lie. Both covers above show it.

Firm colour on the covers and dividers, house palette everywhere else, signal colours never touched?

Decision B — a pale brand must not become unreadable

A cover and a divider carry white text. A firm whose brand is pale yellow would render them illegible, and we cannot see any firm's real colour until Advisor-e supplies it.

Recommended: the page decides from the colour's relative luminance — the firm's colour when it is dark enough to carry white text, the original's navy when it is not. A pale-branded firm gets a navy cover with its logo on it, and nothing is ever unreadable.

Fall back to navy automatically when the firm's colour is too pale for white text?

Decision C — one route, no screen, and ship on the name alone

firmBrand() is built but on no route, which is why nothing binds. Its query is SELECT id, name always, and only adds the logo and colour columns when they are configured — so the firm's real name works today; only the image and the colour wait on the master team.

Recommended: one Restify route serving it; no screen is built, because the values live on the firm profile page in Advisor-e (his 2026-08-15 ruling). Ship on the name alone — the real name and initial appear immediately, the words “Firm logo” disappear from both documents on day one, and the logo and colour arrive later with nothing more to build.

One backend route, no new screen, shipped on the firm's name without waiting for Advisor-e?

Decision D — does the Business Performance Report get this too

It is the same placeholder in the same kind of document, and its own drawing reserved the place in 2026. Leaving it would mean one client document branded and the other captioned — the opposite of the consistency asked for.

Recommended: yes, both. It is the same three values into boxes that already exist, with no layout change to the report at all. The alternative is doing this twice.

Fill the report's fourteen footers and its cover in the same change?
