The five decisions

Decision A — one frame per sheet, never two

A teaching page is almost entirely filled by a concept drawing, and that drawing already carries its own firm-coloured frame — a 10px stroke on its 1500×844 page. Put a firm border on the sheet as well and a client sees two coloured frames a few millimetres apart, with a white channel between them.

Recommended: the firm's border frames every sheet that is not a teaching page — cover, agenda, both dividers, capture pages and the closing page. On a teaching page the drawing's own frame is the firm's border, and the sheet keeps its plain hairline. Every page in the document is then framed in the firm's colour, drawn exactly once. Sheet 4 above shows it.

Is one frame per sheet right, with the teaching page's frame coming from the drawing inside it?

Decision B — the mark sits in the same place on every sheet, except the cover

The 33 drawings put the mark bottom-left: the logo box starts 5.87% in from the left, is 5.21% of the page tall and clears the foot by 2.61%. Those are the drawings' own numbers, not new ones.

Recommended: the plan's own sheets use the same position and the same proportions, so a client turning pages finds the firm in the same spot every time. The cover is the exception — there the mark leads, at the top and 1.6× the size, because it is the first thing anyone sees and the firm should be the first thing on it.

Same place on every sheet, with the cover's mark larger and at the top?

Decision C — the dividers take the firm's colour as the sheet

The step dividers are the only solid-colour pages in the document; they are navy #002b64 today. They are the natural place for a firm's colour to be seen properly rather than as a hairline. But white text on a pale firm colour is unreadable, and we will not know any firm's real colour until Advisor-e supplies it.

Recommended: the divider uses the firm's colour as its background when that colour is dark enough to carry white text, and stays navy when it is not — decided by the page itself from the colour's relative luminance, not by anyone eyeballing it. A firm with a pale yellow brand gets a navy divider with a yellow border and a yellow mark, and nothing is unreadable. Sheet 3 shows the firm-coloured case; sheet 6 the navy one.

Should a divider take the firm's colour when it is dark enough, and stay navy when it is not?

Decision D — where the firm's brand comes from at runtime

firmBrand() is built and reads the firm's row, but it is on no route, so the browser cannot ask for it. That is why the page binds nothing.

Recommended: one new Restify route, GET /api/report/firm/brand, returning { name, logo, colour } for the firm on the caller's token. The page calls it once and binds the three props, plus the initial derived from the name. No screen is built — the values live on the firm profile page in Advisor-e, which is your ruling of 2026-08-15 and unchanged.

One backend route, no new screen?

Decision E — what shows before Advisor-e names their two columns

firmBrand()'s query is SELECT id, name always, and only adds the logo and colour columns when they are configured. So the firm's real name works today; only the logo image and the colour wait on the master team.

Recommended: ship it now. A firm gets its real name and its real initial on every sheet immediately, with the disc and borders in the existing #0070c0 until the colour arrives. The literal words “Firm logo” and the blank circle both disappear on day one, and nothing further is built when Advisor-e answers.

Ship on the name alone, with the logo and colour arriving later with no more work?
