The rule this form follows

  • A column with a name is a thing being compared — a persona, a stage. Its name sits at the head of the column and every box beneath it belongs to it.
  • A row with a name is the attribute being asked for, and it carries down: a named row followed by unnamed rows means that attribute has that many lines.
  • A column of yours that is filled in is your worked example — shown beside the advisor's own column, never typed into. Same rule the banded grid already follows.
  • A column that is blank in your header and blank in every row is a gutter, not a column of the form. Customer Types has one, and today it becomes boxes.

What needs your word — two decisions

The shape of the Customer Types screen is settled by your ruling above and is no longer a question. These two are about the other tables.

A · Your blank separator rows — a gap, or boxes?

Customer & Skills Review has 3 fully blank rows, each sitting between one customer segment and the next. Today each one becomes a box to type into, under no heading.

Recommended: print them as a gap and offer no box. A blank row between groups is how a Word table breathes; read as a question it asks the advisor something you never asked. This takes that screen from 30 boxes to 24.

Should a fully blank row between two groups of your questions print as a gap with no box — instead of continuing to offer a box to type into as it does today? Yes or no.
