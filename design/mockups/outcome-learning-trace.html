The questions as they were put — kept beside the rulings

1 · The cap: at most 10 points, and set aside when the advisor's words matched a distinction?

Your rule is that pooled evidence can never outrank what the advisor said today. The plan makes that two things: a template's pooled hold-back is capped at 10 points in total however many adjustments match; and it is skipped, shown as outweighed, whenever the advisor's words in this session matched one of the firm's Advisory Distinctions for that template. For scale: a distinction boost defaults to 5, and the existing "already delivered to this client" hold-back is 15.

Recommended: yes to both. The cap keeps a hold-back smaller than the client-history one, so the pool never says more than the firm's own history does; the distinction rule is a plain, testable meaning of "what the advisor said today", because a distinction match is the one score the advisor's words produce directly. Against: a template with no distinction can still drop up to 10, which on a close ranking reorders it — but that is the feature, applied only after five firms and the mentor agreed, and the panel shows it happened.

2 · Is the section called "Learned from outcomes"?

The advisor never sees the words "Outcome Learning" or "Outcome Sharing" — those are hub tabs. On the panel the section sits beside "Distinctions" and "How the templates scored", and reads as a plain statement of where the influence came from.

Recommended: "Learned from outcomes". Against: it does not say whose outcomes; the note under it does, and a title long enough to say it would not match its neighbours.

3 · Does an advisor at a sharing firm see the section when nothing applied?

Screen B1: one quiet line, "Nothing learned from other firms applies to this situation yet."

Recommended: yes, at sharing firms only. An advisor who saw a hold-back last week and nothing this week should be able to tell the difference between "nothing matched" and "it broke"; B2 is the broken state and reads differently on purpose. Against: one more line on a panel that is already long; but it is a note, not a row, and it appears only where the firm has chosen to share.

What this drawing does not decide Everything on the mentor's page and the firm's switch is drawings 1 and 2, approved. The reason codes' internal names, the resolver's ordering and the trace block's shape are the plan's, not the screen's, and are pinned by tests rather than by this drawing.
