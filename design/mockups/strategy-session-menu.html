The fourth number is why the AI cannot do this job today, and it is measured in the Brief §6: the recommendation engine's smallest unit is a whole document, so its best answer for a Pivot-shaped client is the two decks — 34 concepts for a session that needs 11. One list, and ticks that cross freely between the panels. Pivot proves the crossing matters: nine of its concepts are in Strategic Orientation 2 and two are in Sales & Marketing, so a menu that makes an advisor choose one domain and stay inside it cannot produce the acceptance test.

The panels are in Mike's own order, which means the advisor meets the two agenda-only documents first. That is deliberate — it is what Decision B is about, and hiding it behind the richer decks would make the screen look better than it is.

3. The decisions — four open

Each is one recommendation and one question a yes or no answers. Asked one at a time in chat; recorded here so the ruling and what was proposed both survive.

Decision A — is the menu your own table, or a new screen?

Your Session Scope table already has the exact column a menu needs: Include (Yes/No). Clients read this table in the room today, on paper.

Recommendation: the screen IS your table, with the Include column made real — same column order, same words, same page numbers.

What this rejects, recorded so it is not re-proposed: a card grid or a redesigned picker. It would look more like an app and less like the thing the advisor and the client are both already holding.

Is the menu your own Session Scope table with the Include column made real?

Decision B — the 18 rows with no description

Three of your five documents are agendas, so 18 of the 52 rows have a name and nothing to read. An advisor scanning the menu can weigh 34 of them and must already know the other 18.

Recommendation: show them as name-only now, and give the mentor a place to write the two lines later — never generated, never filled by AI. A description that shapes what a client is taught is yours to write.

The cost of the alternative is stated plainly: writing 18 × 2 lines is an evening of your time, and nothing can start it but you.

Show those rows as name-only, with the descriptions left for you to write later?

Decision C — how far the AI suggestion goes

The engine cannot propose concepts today (Brief §6). Once this menu exists it can — and the question is what it is allowed to do with the answer.

Recommendation: it pre-ticks and writes one line of reason against each row, and nothing is included until the advisor leaves it ticked. It never unticks, never hides a row, and the count follows the ticks rather than the suggestion.

This keeps the Planner inside the rule the rest of the feature already follows — the AI tidies and proposes, the human decides. A build where the suggestion silently becomes the scope has broken it.

Does the AI pre-tick with a reason against each row, changing nothing by itself?

Decision D — your own diagnostic, on this screen

Sales & Marketing page 4 carries “Where To Start??” — a routing flow that sends an advisor to the right concept. The census found it is the only thing in any deck that answers when a section is necessary.

Recommendation: put it on this screen as a second way in — answer the diagnostic and it ticks for you, on the same rows, which you then adjust. It is your answer to this problem and it predates ours.

Should “Where To Start??” run as an optional route into the ticks?

4. What this drawing does not settle, stated rather than assumed

  • How ticks become steps. That is Decision 2 on the assembled-plan drawing — asked and unanswered: does the advisor name the steps, or does the app group the ticks? This menu produces a tick-list and works either way.
  • A concept ticked into two steps. Pivot uses Porter's twice, on pages 11 and 21. That is Decision 4 on the same drawing. This screen ticks a concept once; visiting it twice belongs to the step screen, not here.
  • The 21 teaching forms and the 9 capture forms. Untouched by this screen and still open (census §7).
  • How a manager adds a concept, at each tier. Mike's request of 2026-09-17, undesigned.
  • Nothing here has met a database. No statement in the Planner's store has ever run against real MySQL — it was written on the laptop, which has none. That is desktop or UAT work.

Two honest limits on the drawing itself

The client is invented. “Ferndale Engineering Ltd” and the date are placeholders, as is the sentence about eight pre-ticked rows — no suggestion has ever been run, because the index it would read does not exist yet.

The ticks are real. The eleven ticked rows are Pivot's actual concepts, so the acceptance test can be checked against this page by eye, today, before anything is built.
