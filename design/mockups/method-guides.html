What your advisors’ AI is taught about this method

Framing a Conflict Meeting · 12,090 characters · every line below goes to the AI

Objective
To equip advisors with the psychological framework and practical facilitation tools to run a conflict meeting — from understanding why people double down on their positions, through creating a cognitive pathway for letting go, to managing the room with status and structured process.
Core principle
The goal of a conflict meeting is not to declare a winner — it is to create an environment where people can catch themselves acting in violation of their own higher values, and choose to let go of their current position for the greater good.

Stage 1Understanding Conflict Psychology

Key principle
Before you can facilitate conflict effectively, you need to understand why people argue the way they do. Reason and logic are not neutral — they are survival tools wired for persuasion.
Coaching points
  • Dan Sperber (evolutionary psychologist): the function of internalised reason and logic is to help us argue — to manipulate and convince others to meet our survival needs, and to be wary when others attempt to do the same to us.
  • Confirmation bias is the proof: people don’t reason to find truth — they reason to find information that supports the position they already hold.

Stage 2Creating the Cognitive Pathway — Detachment

Key principle
People double down on their positions not because they are stubborn — but because letting go feels like a loss of self. Your first job is to create a safe cognitive pathway for them to release their position without losing their identity.
Coaching points
  • To accept new information, a person must first be prepared to let go of their current belief without feeling a loss of self.

4. Why the boxes are not all the same

Measured across all 155,000 characters of the thirteen.

What it isShareConsequence
The four fields all thirteen share21% Objective, core principle, key concepts, additional guidance
The staged sequence44% Called stages, steps, application_steps, step_by_step or pillars, depending on the guide
Blocks unique to one guide35% 86% of Dashboard Discussions · 73% of Working Capital Cycle · 69% of Ratio Analysis

A screen with a fixed set of boxes would leave most of those three guides invisible — this item’s own fault, one level down. The Santa Claus sequence above is the illustration: an array of question objects, inside a coaching stage, inside the document.

So one renderer walks each guide’s own shape. Every authored line becomes a field; the document’s own section names become the headings; nothing is listed by hand in code. A guide with a section nobody anticipated still renders in full — and the same walk builds the prompt.

Structure is fixed; words are editable. A firm may reword any line and cannot add or remove a stage. Editing the shape is authoring a method, and that is the mentor’s work.

What changes for a live adviser, said plainly. The screen takes nothing away — no tier has these controls today. But the AI starts receiving 116 pieces of coaching it has never had, concentrated in Dashboard Discussions, Working Capital Cycle and Ratio Analysis. Those three conversations will move. They should move toward what Mike wrote — that is the point — but “should” is a prediction, and the check is to open a session in each of the three and read what comes back on the running app before it ships.

Which tiers see it is not assumed here. The diagnostic block was ruled mentor only on 2026-08-16 — “this looks too technical for a firm or global manager.” A method guide is ordinary advisory prose rather than routing logic, so that reasoning does not obviously carry across. It is asked, not inherited, once the wording is settled.
