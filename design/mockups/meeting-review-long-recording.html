Recording a long meeting in parts

An ordinary recorded meeting is cut into 20-minute parts by itself. The advisor doesn't have to do anything. Each part is turned into text as soon as it closes, and its audio is deleted straight after. At the end the parts join into one transcript, so My Coaching Notes and the Meeting Summary work exactly as they do now. Words with a yellow background are new on this screen. Everything else is the recorder as it is built today.

The impact test, stated before the design

  • The problem. Any recorded client meeting longer than 23 minutes 20 seconds gets no transcript, and its audio is deleted anyway. OpenAI's speaker-labelling model refuses more than 1400 seconds of audio. That was proven on 1 Oct: a 40-minute recording was refused at 72.8 MB and again at 18.2 MB, so the limit is the length, not the file size. Most client meetings run longer than that.
  • How we'll know.
    • The same 40-minute, two-voice test recording that was refused on 1 Oct comes back as one transcript. All 126 numbered points are present, in order, with no gap at 20:00 or 40:00.
    • The advisor is labelled as the advisor in every part, not only the first. Checked by reading each part of that test.
    • Words lost where one part ends and the next begins are counted at each boundary, not assumed to be none.
    • Later, one real client meeting of an hour or more, checked the same way.
  • What already does this job. The strategy-session recorder already records in parts, labels the advisor in every part, and joins the parts into one transcript. This drawing reuses that machinery. It isn't enough on its own because it hangs each part on a concept card, and an ordinary meeting has no cards.

Decisions for you, one at a time

A · The meeting splits itself at 20 minutes RULED · Mike's yes, 2026-10-01

Recommended: every 20 minutes of recorded audio, a part closes and the next one starts with no gap. The advisor doesn't press anything. There's also a 20 MB cut, whichever comes first, the same as the strategy session you ruled today.

A hard cut can split a sentence, and a word or two at the join may be lost or garbled. OpenAI's own guide warns about this. The test above counts the loss at each join rather than assuming it away. If it's noticeable, the fix is to cut at the first quiet second after 19 minutes. That would be a change for both recorders, and yours to rule then.

B · Each part is turned into text as soon as it closes, and its audio deleted at once RULED · Mike's yes, 2026-10-01

Recommended: yes, as in the strategy session. Audio is held for 20 minutes at most instead of the whole meeting. A failure shows within minutes, and the wait after "Finish meeting" is only the last part.

Not recommended: holding every part until the end and sending them all then. It's simpler, but the audio lives longer and a failure is only found after the client has left.

C · The advisor's 8-second voice clip is used to label the advisor in every part RULED · Mike's yes, 2026-10-01, with the privacy risk below stated to him first

Recommended: yes, exactly as in the strategy session. The clip is cut from the start of the consent line, sent with each part, and deleted when the recording ends. If the meeting ends before 20:00 it is never sent anywhere and is deleted at the end.

Why it's needed: today the app decides who the advisor is by who speaks first, because the advisor reads the consent line. That's only true of part 1. In part 2 the client may speak first, and every label in that part would swap without anyone noticing. The other option considered was matching speakers using a few seconds where the parts overlap. That keeps no voice sample, but nothing like it has been built or tested, and it fails when nobody speaks in the overlap.

🔴 The privacy risk in Decision C, stated before you rule

  • A voice clip kept so software can recognise a person is biometric data, which is special-category under UK and EU law. The Meeting Review Brief (§3) deliberately keeps none for an ordinary meeting, because until now it wasn't needed.
  • You approved this same clip for strategy sessions on 28 Sep, with the risk stated. Saying yes here extends it to every recorded meeting. It's a new decision, not a consequence of that one.
  • What limits it: the clip is the advisor's voice, never the client's. It lives only while the meeting's audio does, and is deleted with logged proof. It goes only to the transcription endpoint, which keeps nothing (ZDR rule Z1).
  • The consent wording isn't changed. It already names AI transcription, and the strategy session uses the same wording with the same clip.

D · The advisor sees the parts RULED · Mike's yes, 2026-10-01

Recommended: yes, from 20:00, as the row on screen 1. It's the only way a failed part shows while the client is still there (screen 2).

Not recommended: hiding the parts and showing one clock only. It's tidier, but a failure in part 1 would stay hidden until the end.

E · A failed part leaves a stated gap. It never fails the whole meeting RULED · Mike's yes, 2026-10-01, for both recorders

Recommended: the parts that worked are kept and joined. The "done" panel names the missing minutes (screen 4), and both reports say which minutes they could not read. They don't quote across the gap as if it weren't there.

⚠ This also applies to the strategy session as built. Its join already records a missing part, but the reports don't read that record yet. Building E fixes both recorders at once.
