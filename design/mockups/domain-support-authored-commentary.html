Marking our own commentary

Domain Support rows are transcriptions of the firm’s own documents. Mixed into them is a second kind of sentence — a short clause explaining why a step matters, written by us, not by the firm. Mike’s ruling of 2026-08-14 is mark them, do not delete them. This is how.

1 · What we are actually marking

Not whole rows, and not whole sentences. Our additions are tails welded onto the end of the firm’s own sentences. That single fact drives every decision below.

3 · Making and removing a mark

You highlight the words in the step and press a button. You never retype them, so a mark can never come adrift from the sentence it points at — the fault the guard test exists to catch simply cannot be created. Removing a mark is the × on the note.

Option 3

Who sees this button

Us, at platform level, for now. The mark says who wrote the words, so it reaches only the words your own level wrote — that is the cascade rule (“a level holds only its changes, never a copy”), not a permission. A firm marking commentary it has written is a real case and will come, but the label above is the platform speaking to a firm and would read wrong in a firm's own voice. That wording is not invented here; it is asked for when a firm first has its own commentary to mark. Until then the notes render read-only at firm level.

A firm that disagrees with a mark is not stuck: editing the sentence clears it, and the words genuinely become theirs at that point. What a firm cannot do is un-tick a mark while keeping our words — that would leave something we know to be false standing at every other firm, and would silently cost that firm every future improvement to the whole area (arrays replace wholesale — Domain Support P4).

6 · What is stored, and the check that keeps it honest

The firm’s text is not touched. A short companion list is added beside each material — that is the entire data change, and it is additive, so nothing that reads these files today alters its behaviour.

Added to a material in data/strategy-domain-support.json

"authored_commentary": [
  {
    "text": "so purchasing blockages are removed rather than competed against",
    "checked": "2026-08-14",
    "searched": "all 115 firm documents — zero matches"
  },
  {
    "text": "which is what captures accumulative incremental growth rather than one large bet",
    "checked": "2026-08-14",
    "searched": "all 115 firm documents — zero matches"
  }
]

The guard test

  • Every marked fragment must still appear, word for word, in its material. If someone edits the sentence and orphans the mark, the suite goes red that day rather than the label quietly pointing at nothing.
  • It must appear exactly once. Two matches means the mark is ambiguous, and the screen would guess which one it meant.
  • Every entry carries the date it was checked and what was searched. A mark with no provenance is an opinion.

There is no step number in the record. The words are the anchor, so reordering the steps — which the screen already allows — cannot break it.

7 · What this does not do

  • It does not find the clauses. Marking is the container; the reading is still done by hand, one domain against its own source PDF. Three detectors were built on 2026-08-14 and all three were defeated by paraphrase.
  • It does not sweep the other 28 domains. Only Strategy has been read. The often-quoted 150–200 figure is an extrapolation from one domain of twenty-nine — it is not a count and must not harden into one.
  • It does not relax the never-invent rule. Inventing the firm’s method stays forbidden. A factual claim about a named framework — an acronym, a list, a count — is never ours to author, marked or not.
