Why it exists. Writing the specification found that the forecast has no country field at all — none on the intake, none in any shared code. Every rule in this feature reading "a client in that country" was pointing at something the app does not know. Mike's ruling, 2026-09-09: ask once per forecast, defaulting to the firm's own country.

Two things follow from that ruling and are drawn below: the country is saved with the forecast, and changing it never silently moves a rate.

2 · Changing it never moves a figure on its own

The same rule Mike already ruled for approvals (advisor drawing §5, ruling 3): figures moving underneath someone part-way through a report is how a number gets into a document nobody meant to send. Switching country is told, counted, and waits.

You changed this client's country to New Zealand — 3 of 6 rates would change Vehicles 20% → 50%, Office equipment 25% → 25% (unchanged), Plant and equipment 22% → 13%. Your forecast has not been altered. The rate you typed for Plant and equipment will be kept.

4 · What this drawing deliberately does NOT do
  • It does not create a client record. The country is a fact about this forecast, saved with it. Mike's ruling took the client-record option off the table precisely because that record does not exist yet and the feature would have waited on it.
  • It does not offer every country in the world as a live choice. A country with no approved table anywhere gets the app's six defaults, badged as defaults — the advisor is never blocked, and never told a country is unavailable.
  • It does not change what a rate means. Mike ruled on 2026-09-09 that the forecast keeps ONE depreciation rate per category, charged as an expense and taxed on the profit after it. Changing country changes that one rate, so it moves reported profit as well as tax — which is why the notice in §2 counts the rows rather than just saying "rates updated".
