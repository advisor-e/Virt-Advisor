Four things to rule before this is built

Each is a real choice, not a detail. Nothing gets built until these are settled.

1 · Three charts, or two?

Charts 1 and 2 each carry a fact no table row makes visible. Chart 3 restates the headline figures in a different shape.

Recommendation: keep charts 1 and 2, cut chart 3. Two charts that each say something new beat three where one repeats.

2 · Where do the charts sit?

Drawn here above the tables, directly under the headline figures — the picture first, the exact figures underneath for the conversation.

Recommendation: above, as drawn. An advisor opens the page to the story, then scrolls to the detail.

3 · Full figures on chart 1, against the house style

Every other chart in the app abbreviates to thousands — $289k. July's margin is -$132, which abbreviates to $0k — and “$0k” destroys the one fact the chart exists to show. Chart 1 is drawn with full figures; charts 2 and 3 keep the house abbreviation.

Recommendation: full figures on chart 1 only, as drawn — a deliberate, recorded deviation, because the alternative prints a false zero.

4 · Printing

These reports are printed and left with a client. Inline SVG prints at full resolution, and the charts carry no interaction to lose on paper.

Recommendation: the charts print with the report, exactly as the tables do. Only the Back button is hidden on paper, which is already the case.
