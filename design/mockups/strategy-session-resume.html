The door — reopening a strategy session

An advisor in a room with a client cannot currently leave this screen and come back to it. If they navigate away for any reason — a calculator, a phone call, the client's own report, a mis-clicked back button, the laptop lid — they return to an empty Scope screen with no client selected, and pressing Build the session again opens a second, blank session for the same client. Everything typed is safely stored; nothing in the app can reach it again.

The two buttons, and what each one actually does

Save session — and the fault it uncovered

Asked what a Save button would do, the honest first answer was nothing: the app was already saving. Chasing that down found it was saving once per keystroke — measured at 62 saves for a 62-character sentence, each one a separate database write, and fired without waiting for one another, so a slow connection could store a half-typed answer. The code's own notes had claimed it saved when you left a box. That is fixed, tested and proved — a box now saves when the advisor leaves it.

Which is what makes your button real rather than decorative. The box being typed into genuinely is not saved yet, so Save session has something to commit — the open box and the scope — and something to confirm.

Leave session — drawn, and not built, because it has nowhere to go yet

Our pages have no navigation of their own — `layouts/default.vue` is four lines, just the page. So the way back out is Advisor-e's menu, and this app does not hold that address.

🔴 WE ARE NOT ASKING ADVISOR-E FOR IT — Mike, 2026-09-22: "we dont need to ask the master coding team the address for main menu - get rid of it and any notes relating to it." A question had been drafted for them and a config seam built for their answer; both are removed and nothing in the repository asks them. Do not re-raise it. How the button gets its destination is a question for Mike.

🔴 Until it has one the button is not rendered at all — the same rule the Suggest for this client button was fixed under on 2026-09-22: a control that looks live and does nothing cannot be told apart from a broken app. It is also not shown on Scope before a session exists, because there is nothing to leave.

It asks no "are you sure?" — a named choice, not an oversight. Everything is saved, the bar on Screen 1 brings the advisor straight back, and a confirmation box in front of a client is a jolt for no gain.

The decisions — five, all settled

One recommendation and one yes/no each, asked one at a time. The recommendation is kept beneath a ruling so a later session can see what was proposed as well as what was decided.

Decision A — what happens when the advisor picks a client who has been here before — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-22. The screen offers to reopen the most recent session, with Start a new session beside it. Never a silent resume. He added two things in the same breath, both drawn above: a Leave session button back to Advisor-e's menu, and Save session at any time.

Recommendation kept: a bar above the scope table naming the most recent session, what is in it, when it was last touched and whose it is — with Reopen this session and Start a new session beside it. Never a silent resume, never a pop-up.

Rejected alternatives, named so they are not re-proposed: silently reopening the most recent (guesses wrong in front of a client and there is no way back); a modal (a jolt mid-meeting, and it blocks an advisor who wants a fresh session); a separate "my sessions" page (a second place to look, when the advisor is already on the screen that knows the client).

When an advisor picks a client who already has a session, should the screen show a bar offering to reopen the most recent one with Start a new session beside it — instead of going straight to a blank new session as it does today? Yes or no.

Decision B — where reopening puts the advisor — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-22. Reopening takes the advisor straight back to the concept they were working on, not to the beginning. The green banner naming where they are, and the one click back to Scope, are what make that safe rather than disorienting — they are part of the ruling, not decoration.

Recommendation kept: back at the concept they last had open, on Run session, with the green banner naming it and one click back to Scope.

It costs nothing to build. The session's timeline already records every field opened and closed, newest last, so the last concept touched is known — and a concept with an open field can only have been open on Run session. ⚠ The stage itself is not stored anywhere, so a session with no timeline at all (scoped but never run) opens on Scope, which is where its advisor stopped.

Against it: an advisor who has forgotten what they were doing lands deep in the session rather than at the beginning. The banner is the answer to that, and the rail has been clickable in any order since your ruling of 2026-09-21, so nothing is trapped.

Asked as: when you reopen a session, should it take you straight back to the concept you were working on — instead of starting you at the beginning on the first screen?

Decision C — whether a session ever ends — DROPPED, AGAINST THE RECOMMENDATION

🔴 DROPPED — Mike, 2026-09-22, and he was right. His challenge: "each session relates to a single client - tell me why i need this feature." The bar only appears once a client is chosen, so the app is never choosing between people — only between that client's own sessions, newest first. The bar names the date and what is in it, so an advisor sees at a glance whether it is this week's work or last quarter's, and one click on Start a new session settles it. That is the whole cost of not having this.

Against it, and this is why the recommendation was wrong: a "finished" state has to be SET, by a person, at the moment a meeting breaks up and the client is putting their coat on. It would be forgotten more often than not — and a record that is confidently wrong about what is finished is worse than no record. ⚠ Nothing is lost. If it ever matters, it can be DERIVED rather than remembered: producing the client's plan is the natural end of a session and the app already knows when that happened.

Recommendation kept, so a later session can see what was proposed as well as what was decided: the advisor marks a session finished from Produce plan, and a finished session stops being the one the bar offers while staying fully readable and reopenable in the earlier-sessions list.

⚠ The reason this is a decision and not a detail. Today a session's status is set to open when it is created and nothing in the app ever changes it. Every session a client has ever had is open. Nothing is harmed by that now, because nothing reads it — but the moment the bar exists, it has to know which session it means, and after a year of quarterly planning a regular client would have several equally open ones.

If the answer is no, the bar works on recency alone and always offers the newest. That is workable and cheaper; the cost is that a session finished months ago keeps being offered until a newer one replaces it.

Asked as: should you be able to mark a session as finished, so it stops being the one offered when you come back — while still being there to open whenever you want it? Answered by dropping it.

Decision D — whether the "Saved" stamp is always on screen, or only after pressing Save — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-22: "yes - auto save". The stamp is always on screen and updates itself, and saving is something the advisor never has to think about. Save session stays as the deliberate act, for an advisor who wants to be certain before they stand up.

Recommendation kept: always on screen, updating itself — the quiet green Saved 2:41pm drawn beside the two buttons on Screen 2, which moves on every automatic save as well as on a press of Save session.

The reason is the request itself. Wanting a Save button is not really about saving — the app was already doing that. It is about being able to see that your client's words are safe, which a button you have to press cannot tell you until you press it. A stamp that is simply there answers the question before it is asked, and it costs one line on the screen.

Against it: one more thing on a screen an advisor uses in front of a client. It is small, grey and in the corner for exactly that reason.

Asked as: should the session show a quiet Saved 2:41pm stamp at all times, updating itself as answers save — instead of confirming a save only when Save session is pressed?

Decision E — what the stamp says when there ARE words not yet stored — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-22. The stamp admits it. While the advisor is still typing and their words have not been saved, it reads Unsaved changes for those few seconds, rather than always showing Saved with a time beside it.

⚠ Decision D creates this and it cannot be left to the build. A box saves when the advisor leaves it. So while they are typing into the box in front of them, those words are not stored yet — and a stamp reading Saved 2:41pm beside three freshly typed sentences is not reassurance, it is a false statement at the one moment it matters. A stamp that only ever says "Saved" is worse than no stamp at all.

Recommendation: the stamp tells the truth in three states — Saved 2:41pm when everything is stored, Saving… for the moment it takes, and Unsaved changes while there are words in the open box that have not reached the database.

And the app keeps "Unsaved changes" short rather than leaving it hanging, which is what "auto save" has to mean: the open box is written out after a short pause in typing, not only when the advisor leaves it. That is what closes the real hole — an advisor who types an answer and then walks away without clicking anything else. ⚠ This is a pause, never a keystroke: per-keystroke saving is the defect fixed on 2026-09-22 and must never return.

Against it: the advisor occasionally sees the words Unsaved changes, which reads as a warning. It is one, briefly, and truthfully — and the alternative is a green tick that lies.

Asked as: while you're still typing and your words haven't been saved yet, should the marker say Unsaved changes for those few seconds — instead of always saying Saved with a time next to it?

What this drawing does NOT decide

  • Nothing about the calculators. Your 2026-09-16 patchwork ruling is untouched and still governs how a calculator is built if one ever goes in a card. Brief section 7a.
  • Nothing about producing the client's plan. That already works and is untouched here. ⚠ It is named because Decision C, dropped, said that if a session ever DOES need an end, it is derived from the plan being produced rather than flagged by hand — that would be a new decision, not something this drawing already allows.
  • Whether the session id belongs in the address bar. It should, so that a refresh returns rather than starting again — but that is invisible plumbing with no screen to approve, and it follows from Decision A either way.
  • Where Leave session actually goes. The button's place on the screen is drawn here; its destination is not, and it is still open. See the note beside the button above.
  • Anything about how often a box saves. That was a defect and it is already fixed, not a choice being put to you. It appears here only because it is what makes Save session meaningful.
