Why this screen exists at all. The Brief §3: the pre-set is what converts an unreliable judgement into a checkable one. Asking a model "how did this advisor do?" produces fluent invention; asking it "find where they framed the meeting, quote it, or answer NOT FOUND" is a search with a citation. 2 Consent, step one — before recording starts ✅ Approved wording

Before you record

Read these words aloud as soon as recording starts. The recording is your evidence that you asked.

✅ Approved 2026-09-01 · read aloud, by you, first "Before we begin — I'd like to record our meeting. The recording is turned into a written transcript by AI, and the recording itself is deleted as soon as that's done. It gives you a written summary of what we agree today, and it helps me review how I did. Nothing is shared outside our firm. Is everyone here happy for me to record?"

The audio is transcribed and then deleted. The transcript is kept for 18 months and is visible to you and your firm — never to Advisor-e.

18 months — your ruling of 2026-09-01. Long enough to clear an annual meeting cycle with six months to spare, so year-on-year follow-through works; short enough that a client hearing it spoken does not pause.

⚠ The number must not be typed into the sentence when this is built. A firm can move its own retention dial, and this line quotes that figure back to a client out loud. 18 months is the platform default, not a constant — a build that hardcodes it has advisors saying something untrue the day a firm changes the setting, and nothing on screen would say so.

This is the correction you caught, drawn as a screen. The order is record, then speak, then confirm. An earlier draft ticked "I have read the consent line aloud" before recording began — past tense, which puts the client's agreement outside the audio and defeats the whole point of it. The tick is your undertaking; the audio is the evidence. They are not interchangeable.
  • Repeating the line is deliberate — it stops an advisor paraphrasing from memory and paraphrasing the AI clause out of existence.
  • It also anchors who is who. You speak first, so the software knows which voice is the advisor's — no stored voice sample, so no biometric data.

Three things this screen is carrying.
  • "Stop and delete" never leaves the screen. It is the answer to a client who says "actually, can you turn that off?" — and it destroys the audio and any transcript already made from it.
  • Audio is sent away in short pieces as it is captured, so a crash costs seconds rather than the meeting. There is no second take with a real client.
  • The list stays visible — you chose live capture over uploading a file, and this is part of what that buys.
Recording stopped unexpectedly at 42:19 Your screen locked or the browser tab was suspended. 42 minutes were saved and can still be transcribed. Nothing after 42:19 was captured.
The alarm above is the honest half of your live-capture ruling. Uploading a file was the safer recommendation and you chose live capture, which is the right call for consent and speed — but an operating system can suspend a backgrounded tab, and a mid-meeting screen lock is not rare. The rule is that this fails loudly: a tidy page of "no observations" must never be what a total failure looks like.

Four rules are visible on this screen, and each one is load-bearing.
  • Every finding quotes the transcript or declares the thing absent. An observation with no citation is thrown away rather than shown. "NOT FOUND" has to be as easy for the model to answer as a quote, or it will invent evidence to be helpful.
  • You can disagree with any of it, and the disagreement stays in the record. This is the line between coaching and surveillance — and it is the only honest source of data for improving the observation points themselves.
  • The report admits what it cannot know. The grey card is the drawing check, ruled on 2026-09-01: your words raise it, the card says out loud that it is guessing, and you settle it in one tap. What gets stored is your answer, never the guess — otherwise a maybe would harden into a fact on its way to the manager's figures.
  • It is yours. No manager at any tier reads this unless you send it.

This second screen is the one most firms will actually meet, which is why it is drawn. Your ruling of 2026-09-01: 5 advisors and 20 meetings before any figure appears.
  • The cost is real and you accepted it. A four-advisor firm sees no manager figures, ever. They still set the observation points, and their advisors still get their own notes — only this one panel stays empty.
  • The empty state has to explain itself. If it just shows nothing, the first manager to open it reports a bug, and the honest answer is that the software is protecting their people.
  • It is not a tuning constant. Lowering it to make a screen look populated would undo the promise, quietly, and no test would catch it.
