3. The reading proposed

A named-field stack is a set of named fields, each with one box

  • Your field names are printed, never typed into. Whether they run across the top row or down the first column, a name is a label.
  • One box per field. Where your table has blank lines, the box is the line beneath the name. Where it has none, the box is the cell beneath the heading.
  • Your worked example sits inside its own field’s box, as grey guide text the advisor types over — the same treatment the persona grid already uses, and never a heading of its own.
  • The fields are separated by a gap, and your thin blank rows are that gap — never drawn as rows of their own. Mike’s ruling, 2026-09-22: “we need a gap between content rows on the productive habits but the additional small row spaces can be deleted.”

Decision 1 — Plan: has no line to write onRuled 2026-09-22

Mike ruled YES, 2026-09-22 — as recommended. Plan: gets a box to write in, like Reason, Trigger, Micro Habit and Effective Practice. So Productive Habits captures five fields, five boxes. The recommendation and the argument against it are left in place below, unedited.

Your four other fields each have a blank row beneath them. Plan does not — it is the last row of the table and your document simply ends there. So either the screen gives Plan a box like its four siblings, which is one box your document does not itself rule; or Plan is shown with your worked example and never captured, in which case the client reads the habit through to a plan and writes nothing down.

Recommended: give Plan a box like the other four. The four fields build to it — Reason, Trigger, Micro Habit, Effective Practice, then Plan — so it is the one field the session exists to produce, and a habit with no plan written against it is the page’s own conclusion left blank. It also costs nothing if you disagree: it is one row.
