Fix 1 — a facility that carries a balance instead of paying it off

What is wrong today

Revolving finance — trade finance, a stock facility, invoice finance — has no home. It sits in Other current liability, which the forecast carries forward unchanged and charges nothing on. On the client this was found with, that is 2,450,000, or 42% of total liabilities, costing the forecast nothing at all. A lender reads a profit that is too high by the whole of the interest.

🔴 AND THE OBVIOUS WORKAROUND IS WORSE, WHICH IS WHY IT IS DRAWN HERE. “Put it in a loan row and set the repayment to zero” does not give an interest-only facility. The loan schedule works capital repaid out as repayment − interest, so a zero repayment makes it negative and the debt grows by its own interest — while the interest is also paid in cash. The charge lands twice and the balance sheet still ties, so nothing complains.

Run on the real engine, not reasoned about: opening 2,450,000 → closing 2,653,348 after twelve months, capital repaid in month 1 −16,333, and 21,913 of interest paid out of the bank in the same month. The debt grew by 203,348 and the year's interest was charged twice.

The funding table proposed

How this is funded One new column
Name itKindOpening balance Monthly repaymentInterest rate

A facility carries its balance and charges interest on it. It does not amortise, so the repayment box is not a box — it says why. Drawdowns and repayments are entered month by month, exactly as a term loan's already can be.

Why one extra column and not a new kind of row. Everything a facility needs — an opening balance, a rate, monthly drawdowns and repayments, a place in the cash flow, a line in the P&L — the funding table already has. The single difference is whether the balance amortises. A second table would be a second thing to learn, a second place to look for the total, and a second set of tests, to express one yes-or-no.

In the engine it is loanSchedule with the amortisation line removed — about fifteen lines, the same shape as code the golden set already proves.

Fix 1 — what is needed from Mike

  1. Does a facility revolve by itself, or does the advisor type the drawdowns and repayments? Recommended: typed. It is how term loans already work, and a facility that redraws itself against stock is a modelling judgement wearing the clothes of a convenience — it would decide, invisibly, how much a client borrows.
  2. How many funding rows should a firm get? Recommended: six. It is what the one real client examined needs, and the app currently folds the surplus into the last row and warns — which is honest, and still a figure the advisor has to unpick by hand.
  3. The label for the kind. Drawn as Term loan / Facility. Alternatives seen in practice: Revolving credit, Trade finance, Interest only. Ours are placeholders.
  4. The words in the repayment box for a facility. Drawn as “not repaid”. It has to say something, because an empty disabled box reads as broken.
  5. Does a facility's interest join the term-loan interest line in the P&L, or get its own? Recommended: its own line. A funding request is read by the lender being asked, and the cost of working-capital finance is the number they look for.

The rate is not a decision, it is an input. No accounting export carries an interest rate, so the advisor types it — exactly as they do for a term loan today.

Fix 2 — deposits already paid on stock that has not arrived

What is wrong today

Money already paid to overseas suppliers for stock still on the water sits in Other current asset — 825,628.98 on the client this was found with. The cash is right and the profit is right. What is wrong is that it never becomes stock: the containers land during the year and the balance sheet still shows the money as a deposit at the end of it.

✅ THE MACHINERY FOR THIS ALREADY EXISTS AND IS PROVED. The overseas section built for item 4.64 already holds a deposit as an asset and releases it into stock in the month the container lands — that is prepaymentClosing. It only ever handles shipments created inside the forecast, because it opens at zero:

const prevPrepaid = m === 0 ? 0 : out.prepaymentClosing[m - 1]

That 0 is the whole of the engine gap. An opening balance flows down the existing release path exactly as a deposit the forecast created itself.

What it does to the three statements. In the landing month the deposit is released out of prepayments and into stock, the balance owing is paid, and the full landed cost joins inventory. Nothing touches the profit and loss until that stock sells, which is the same path the overseas section already takes. The opening cash is untouched — that money left before the forecast began, and the forecast has never counted it.

Fix 2 — what is needed from Mike

  1. Does the balance still owing come across with the deposit? Recommended: yes. Without it the stock lands cheaper than it was bought for and the cash flow misses the payment. This is the ruling that makes the fix a real job rather than a one-line change, and it is said here rather than discovered during the build.
  2. One landing month, or spread across several? Recommended: spread, as drawn. An importer with 825,000 on the water rarely has it on one ship, and a single month would push a whole quarter's stock into one column.
  3. Step 2 with the opening position, or step 3 on the overseas card? Recommended: step 2. It is an opening-balance-sheet figure and it arrives from the file; step 3 is where the advisor forecasts, and this is not a forecast.
  4. Every label in the block. Drawn as “Stock already paid for, not yet arrived”, “Deposits paid, goods not yet landed”, “Still owed to the supplier on landing”, “Arriving in”. All ours. None has been put to him.
  5. Should the months be refused when they do not add up to the deposit? Recommended: warn, do not block. It is the opening position, not a profile that silently loses a fifth of the cash — and the balance check on step 2 already catches the consequence.
The guard, and it is written first for both
With the new fields empty, the forecast must produce byte-identical output across all 10,155 cells of the golden set, and the three statements must be unchanged to the cent. It is written and seen to pass before either fix is built, not after.

Both changes reach the balance sheet and the cash flow, which is exactly the condition that made this guard mandatory for item 4.64. Fix 1 touches the funding schedules, the interest paid out of the bank and the P&L. Fix 2 touches inventory, prepayments, creditors and the cash flow in the landing month.

The second guard is already in place and both fixes must clear it: threeWayForecastIntake.component.test.js fails the build if the engine takes an input the screen does not send. Neither fix can add a field to the engine and forget the screen.

And the third is new as of 2026-09-05: a balance sheet that ties in the accounting package must tie here. Fix 2 moves money between three balance-sheet lines in the landing month, so that assertion is the one most likely to catch an error in it.
