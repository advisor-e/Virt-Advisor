5 · The three rulings — Mike, 2026-09-08
  • 1 · Where the advisor's upload lives. ✅ On this step, beside the rates themselves — not on the client record and not in a menu. The advisor meets the gap here, mid-forecast; a screen they have to go and find is a screen they will not find.
  • 🔴 2 · An advisor MAY type a rate — the recommendation was overturned. The recommendation was that the rate boxes stay read-only, on the grounds that a typed rate with no source reaches a lender looking identical to a sourced one. Mike ruled the other way, and the app already had the answer: every figure on this screen is tagged from file or entered and shows which. A typed rate is an entered figure like any other, not a new concept — badged "entered by you" on screen AND in the print, applying to that one client, never altering the firm's approved table. An advisor who knows the right rate can use it; a reader can still see whose figure it is.
  • 3 · The manager approves while a forecast is open. ✅ Nothing changes on its own. The advisor is told and chooses to apply it, as drawn in §3. Figures moving underneath someone part-way through a report is how a number gets into a document nobody meant to send. And applying leaves typed rates alone, filling only those still on defaults — a rate an advisor deliberately entered is a decision, not a gap.

What this screen deliberately does NOT let an advisor do — each one follows from Mike's ruling that only the firm manager approves:

  • Approve rates. Not shown, not reachable, and refused by the route's role gate — not merely hidden on the screen.
  • Edit the firm's approved table. They may change a rate for their own client, tagged entered — ruled 2026-09-08 — but that figure stays with that client and no other advisor, client or forecast sees it.
  • See another firm's documents. The firm comes from the verified token, never from the browser — the existing rule against cross-firm access.
  • Send anything about the client to the AI. Only the tax document is read. Tax PDFs are public, so no personal-data question arises.
