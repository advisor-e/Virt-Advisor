What this says

This property costs the client $929 a week for the first seven years. It turns cash-positive in year 8, once the principal-and-interest loan is repaid — then dips again in year 9, when the interest-only loan converts and starts repaying principal.

Over ten years it builds $517,407 of equity against $621,474 of money put in — a return of −16.7%. The property has not yet paid back what the client has put into it, and on these assumptions it does not do so inside ten years.

No tax is payable at all: the rental losses are ring-fenced and carried forward, the interest deduction phases out over the first five years, and the loan still being serviced keeps the carried-forward loss alive.
