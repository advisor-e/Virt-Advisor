What this screen is for, in one sentence: a firm manager decides whether their firm's case-review ticks — never their words — join a platform-wide pool that teaches the engine which templates keep not landing, and can undo that decision at any time.

🔴 THE SWITCH IS THE WHOLE GATE. Nothing from a firm reaches the pool unless the switch is on at the moment an advisor saves a review; a firm whose switch is off never receives an adjustment either (spec FR-002, FR-012). There is no second condition and no default-on.

Your firm's choice

Not sharing

When your advisors record how each template went, those ticks can help every firm on the platform — including yours — get better recommendations. Advisor-e pools them, without names, to learn which templates keep not landing in which situations, and holds those back a little for everyone who takes part.

What leaves your firm

  • The advisory domain, and the primary issue where one was confirmed
  • The client's industry — only where it matches Advisor-e's own industry list
  • The signals the engine detected, the engagement type, the staircase step
  • The template titles, and each template's two ticks: Used fully / Partly used / Didn't use it, and Landed well / Didn't land
  • The month of the review

What never leaves

  • Your firm's name, your advisors, your clients, the case
  • Anything anybody typed — the "what went well", "what went less well" and "suggested changes" notes stay at your firm
  • The conversation, the summary, the client's figures
  • Any identifier of any kind
What you get back Where enough firms — at least five, across at least twenty-five cases — have seen the same template not land in the same situation, and the mentor has accepted it, that template ranks a little lower for your advisors in that situation. It never ranks higher because of the pool, and it never outranks what your advisor tells the engine on the day. Every such adjustment is shown on the advisor's "Why this?" panel with the number of firms and cases behind it.
I act for and on behalf of my firm when I choose to share our anonymised template outcomes with Advisor-e to improve recommendations for every participating firm. I understand what leaves our firm and what never does, as set out above, and that I can stop sharing at any time.

Stopping is immediate and needs no reason. What was already shared stays in the pool unless you also ask for it to be withdrawn — that is a separate step, and it is always available.

The questions as they were put — kept beside the rulings

1 · What is the tab called?

Drawn as Outcome Sharing. A firm manager sees a switch about giving something away, so the name says what they are doing, not what the platform does with it.

Recommended: "Outcome Sharing". Against: the mentor's tab (drawing 2) will be called something like "Outcome Learning", and two names for one feature costs a moment's translation between a firm and the mentor. Kept anyway, because the firm never sees the learning and the mentor never flips the switch.

2 · Is the consent sentence right?

The sentence in the box above. First person, an authority clause, and "as set out above" so the record shows the two columns were in front of the person who agreed.

Recommended: approve it as drawn, or replace it with your own words — either way it is stored verbatim with every consent record and pinned. Against: it is four lines; a shorter sentence reads faster, but would have to drop either the authority clause or the "I can stop" clause, and both are load-bearing.

3 · Does a sharing firm see how many adjustments currently apply to it?

The green notice on Screen B: "2 adjustments accepted by the mentor currently apply at your firm." A count only — never the list, which is the mentor's.

Recommended: yes, the count. It answers "is this doing anything?" without giving a firm a second screen to maintain, and it costs nothing because the engine loads those adjustments anyway. Against: a firm seeing "0" for months may switch off; but that is a true statement about a young pool, and hiding it would not make the pool older.

4 · Is the advisor's line right, and is it in the right place?

"Your firm shares these ticks, without names or notes, to improve recommendations across Advisor-e. Nothing you type below leaves the firm." Placed between the ticks and the text boxes.

Recommended: approve as drawn. The position is the message — it draws the line where the data line is. Against: it is one more line on a panel advisors fill in often; but it appears only at sharing firms, and an advisor who has never been told their ticks travel would be right to be annoyed to find out later.

What this drawing does not decide, so nobody reads a decision into it The floor, the direction and the "no free text" rule are Mike's rulings already, quoted in the header, and are not re-asked. The mentor's page and the trace line are drawings 2 and 3. Whether the two middle tiers get a view is answered by the spec (FR-014: no — the pool is one platform-wide set, and a firm's only lever is this switch), and is not a question here.
Which tiers get this, and why

The firm tier alone. Consent is the firm's own undertaking, given by a person who can bind the firm, in the same way its Compliance declaration is. The mentor and the two middle tiers have nothing to switch: they contribute no reviews and they receive no adjustment. The mentor's screen — what was learned, and accept / hold / reject — is drawing 2 and sits at the mentor tier alone (spec FR-014, stated).

The advisor gets one line, not a screen. They are told, at the exact point where the shared ticks end and the private notes begin. Whether an advisor can opt out individually is not in this release, and the spec says so.
