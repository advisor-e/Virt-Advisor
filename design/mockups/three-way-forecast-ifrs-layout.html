The screen keeps all twelve months and the three years, as today; this drawing shows the year total only. "Total other income" goes: its lines now sit where the standard puts them — other income and asset sales above the operating surplus, interest earned below it.
Why each row sits where it does. IFRS 18.69 requires three subtotals: operating profit, profit before financing and income taxes, and profit. Interest on borrowings is financing (IFRS 18.60), and so is interest on other liabilities such as overdue tax and a supplier's charge for waiting to be paid (IFRS 18.61). Interest earned on cash and on loans to shareholders is investing (IFRS 18.53-54), which is why it sits between the operating surplus and the financing section. Interest — overseas suppliers is new because that charge is inside today's Overheads total with no row of its own, so for an importer the lines above the total did not add up to it.

Money going out is shown with a minus, so each group adds down to its own subtotal and the three subtotals add to the movement. The two totals "Money in" and "Money out" are replaced by the three group subtotals on this view.
Why each row sits where it does. IAS 7.10 groups cash flows as operating, investing and financing. Buying and selling assets is investing (IAS 7.16); borrowing, repaying and shareholders' money in and out is financing (IAS 7.17). Tax is operating (IAS 7.35). IAS 7.31-33 let interest paid be financing and interest received be investing, which matches where the profit and loss now puts them — the same interest never sits in two different sections of the two statements.
