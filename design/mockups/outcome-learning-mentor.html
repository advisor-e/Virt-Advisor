The questions as they were put — kept beside the rulings

1 · Does a typed industry enter the pool at all?

An advisor types the client's industry as free text — "cafe", "dairy farm", "Smith & Co panelbeaters". Your rule is that no free text enters the pool. The engine already keeps its own industry vocabulary for scoring templates.

Recommended: industry enters only when the typed value matches a term in that vocabulary, exactly; anything else carries no industry, and the review still contributes on its other dimensions. Against: a firm whose advisors type "café" with an accent, or "coffee shop", contributes no industry rows — but that is the price of never letting a typed name through, and the vocabulary can grow on the Domain Support tab where it already lives.

2 · What is the mentor's tab called?

Drawn as Outcome Learning — the item's own name, and the thing the mentor is looking at: what the platform learned from outcomes. The firm's tab is Outcome Sharing (ruled on drawing 1), because the firm shares and the mentor learns.

Recommended: "Outcome Learning". Against: two names for one feature; kept because they name two different acts by two different people.

3 · Is a reason required to reject, and optional to hold?

Screen B. A rejection overrules the evidence and the next mentor should know why; a hold is a pause.

Recommended: required to reject, optional to hold. Against: a required box gets filled with "no" by a mentor in a hurry — true, and still better than nothing, because the reason travels with the decision into history.

4 · Is a template that crossed the floor with nothing to hold back shown at all?

The Price Rise row: 26 deliveries in education engagements across 5 firms, none marked "Didn't land". The pool has learned something about it, but there is nothing to adjust.

Recommended: show it, hold-back 0, so the mentor sees the reassuring findings as well as the worrying ones — it is the only place the good news appears, since the pool never lifts a template. Against: rows that do nothing add length; on a mature pool most templates will sit at 0, and a filter "show only hold-backs" is a one-line addition when that day comes.

What this drawing does not decide The size of the cap (10 points) and how an applied or outweighed adjustment reads on the advisor's "Why this?" panel are drawing 3. The floor, the direction, "no free text", one dimension per row, and recompute-on-open are your rulings already and are applied here, not re-asked.
Which tiers get this, and why

The mentor tier alone (spec FR-014, a stated judgement under the hub-page rule's default). The pool is one platform-wide set: a global group manager or group manager would see exactly the same rows and could take no different decision on them, and a firm holds no value of its own here — its only lever is the switch on drawing 1, and its only view is the count of adjustments applying to it (ruled there). Cascading becomes mandatory the day a lower tier has a real reason to hold a different value; none has one today.
