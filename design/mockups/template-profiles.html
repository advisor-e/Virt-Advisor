Template Profiles, drawing 4 of 4 — the Mentor Hub tab. Drawn 2026-09-14 for Mike's approval. Nothing is built. Drawings 1–3 (the advisor's primary-issue question, the lift on the Outcome Learning page, and the trace line) are separate files.

What this page is for, in one sentence: the engine ranks templates by matching each one's profile — the list of client problems it answers, each with a weight — against the problems it hears in the case; today that list was generated by a script from the content summaries, nobody can see it, and 49 of the 199 templates have a list too thin to be found. This tab shows every profile, flags the thin ones with the reason, and lets the mentor tick the problems a tool answers and set how strongly.

What it is NOT (Mike's two checks, answered). It is not Advisory Distinctions, which starts from a phrase an advisor says and names the tools it points to; this starts from a tool and names the problems it answers. It is not the Logic-Lab Report, which pushes edits out of the lab. And it never touches the search-content export, which is edited only in Advisor-e and carries no signals at all: the profile is a layer this app keeps on top of it.

Every title, section, signal, weight and summary sentence is real, read from the data files on 2026-09-14. Everything marked example is invented: the dates, the mentor's name, and the one authored row (there are none yet).

Open questions — four, one recommendation each

1 · Is the tab called "Template Profiles"?

"Profile" is the engine's word for the list of problems a template answers, and the spec, plan and tasks all use it. The alternative is plainer: "What each tool answers".

Recommended: "Template Profiles". It sits beside "Template Library" and reads as its pair, and it is the word every document and log line already uses, so a mentor reading a scoring trace finds the same word on the screen. Against: "profile" means nothing to a mentor on day one, and "What each tool answers" says what the screen does — but it is long for a tab and would leave the trace and the tab speaking different languages.

2 · Are the three thin reasons right as worded?

Drawn as No summary (38 — nothing to generate from), No signals matched (8 — a summary exists but the script found no problem in it), Weak match (3 — one signal, weights adding up to under 4, shown beneath as "weights add up to 3").

Recommended: keep the three as drawn. Each names what the mentor must do next: write a summary elsewhere, tick from the summary, or raise a weight. Against: "Weak match" and "No signals matched" are both really "the script could not do it", and one word — "Needs you" — would be simpler; kept as three because the fix for each is different.

3 · Does a freshly ticked signal start at 5?

Screen B: ticking "sales volume" shows 5. The generated profiles use 10 for the main problem and 1–7 for the rest; the engine multiplies the weight in, so 10 is twice the pull of 5.

Recommended: 5. A tick is "this tool answers that problem"; how strongly is the second decision, and a middle value makes the mentor take it rather than inherit it. Against: 10 makes a tick decisive on its own and matches what the script gives the main problem — a mentor who ticks and never touches the box gets a template that ranks like the generated ones. Ruled by Mike as "a standard value"; 5 or 10 is the choice.

4 · Does the table list all 199, or only the client-facing tools?

The profile file holds 199 entries and the engine scores every one, including External Advisors, Firm Manager Access, Help and Programme pages — 38 of which have no summary because they were never written as answers to a client's problem.

Recommended: all 199 — they are the entries the engine scores, and a page the engine can recommend but the mentor cannot see is the gap this screen exists to close. The "Thin only" filter and the section column keep the odd ones together. Against: a mentor scrolling past Insurance and PDF Storage may reasonably ask why they are on a list of advisory tools; the honest answer is that they are scored today, and "authored as none" is how a mentor says so.

Which tiers get this, and why

The mentor tier alone (a stated judgement under the hub-page rule's default, and spec story 9 scenario 3). The profile is platform content: it describes what a template is for, and that does not change from firm to firm. A firm's own vocabulary — the words its advisors use — already reaches scoring through Advisory Distinctions, which every tier has. Cascading becomes mandatory the day a firm has a real reason to hold a different fingerprint for the same template; none has one today, and the store this plan uses (one overlay row per template, scoped) can carry a firm's row without a schema change when that day comes.
