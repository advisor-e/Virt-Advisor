3 · What this actually grants

Read before ruling §4. This screen is not data entry; it hands a real person real visibility.

Placing a firm in a global group gives that group's manager sight of that firm's people. The Adviser Network tab already rolls up by tier — the server works out the caller's level and returns their slice. So the moment Hartley & Vine sits under Advisor-e UK, the Advisor-e UK manager sees Hartley & Vine's advisers through that tab. That is the intended behaviour of a management tier, and it is exactly why the act of placing a firm has to be deliberate, attributed and reversible rather than a quiet dropdown.
  • Content flows down. The group manager's edits reach every firm in the group that has not changed the same thing itself. A firm's own change still wins and sticks.
  • Nothing new flows up. This screen adds no reporting. Team Progress and Team Case Studies stay firm-only, as ruled.
  • Reversible. Moving a firm out returns it to inheriting from you. Its own edits are untouched throughout — they were never a copy of anyone's content.

4 · Open decisions — for Mike to rule

Five. Each carries a recommendation, so a "yes to all" is a valid answer.

Open — 2026-08-10

  1. 1 · What is this called? The screen is drawn as Global Groups, and a firm is placed in a group. Recommendation: keep both. "Group" alone collides with Collaborate's Special Interest Groups, which are a different thing entirely, and "assign" reads like a task rather than a structure. Your call on the words.
  2. 2 · Country — now, or not yet? The tier chain supports a country level between global group and firm. This screen does not offer it. Recommendation: not yet. You asked for the global group; adding country now doubles the screen and the testing for a tier nobody can log into either. The chain already handles a firm whose group is known but whose country is not — it simply inherits from the global group. Adding country later changes this screen, not the engine.
  3. 3 · Can a firm be in two global groups? Recommendation: no — one group, one parent. Two parents means two different answers to "whose content do I inherit?", and no rule for choosing that would not be arbitrary. The dropdown enforces it by shape.
  4. 4 · Does placing a firm need a confirm step? Given §3, recommendation: yes — a short confirm naming the consequence ("The Advisor-e UK manager will be able to see Hartley & Vine's advisers"), plus the who-and-when stamp shown in the table. Every change is versioned and restorable regardless, because this reuses the same storage as everything else on the Hub. Say if that reads as friction and you would rather it just save.
  5. 5 · Who may open this screen? Recommendation: the mentor only. A global manager editing their own membership would let a tier enlarge its own reach — the same reason the underlying function refuses to be called from a request path. If a global manager should be able to see their group's firm list read-only, that is a small addition and worth saying now rather than later.

5 · How it is stored — no new database table

Recorded here because it is the reason this is a small build rather than a schema change, and because a schema change would put us back behind the master team.

  • The membership map is stored through the same overlay mechanism every Hub tab already uses, under one new key, against the reserved mentor scope. Version history and restore come for free — the same machinery that already versions your Advisory Distinctions.
  • Each global group also needs its own reserved row in the firms table, exactly as __platform__ already has one. Without it the database refuses the group's saved content while the dev fallback still reports success — the precise trap that ran the mentor's own saves broken for weeks. Creating a group creates its row, or creating the group fails loudly. It will not be allowed to half-succeed.
  • If the master team later adds a group column to the firms table, theirs becomes the source and this becomes the override. Nothing here is thrown away and nothing has to be migrated.

6 · What will still not be proven when this ships

Written before the build rather than discovered after it.

  • No one can still log in as a global manager. Advisor-e's login issues no such role. This screen makes the tier reachable — content authored at group level will genuinely flow to the firms placed in the group — but it is you, at mentor level, who will be able to demonstrate that, not a group manager at their own screen.
  • It will be evidenced by tests, not by a live login. Same limitation as the tier chain itself, and the second thing to re-check when the real roles arrive.
  • The Adviser Network roll-up described in §3 has never met real tier data. It resolves the caller's tier server-side and is written to roll up, but no global manager has ever called it. That is a statement about what is untested, not a doubt about what it does.
