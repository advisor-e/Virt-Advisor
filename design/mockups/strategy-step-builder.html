The Step Builder

The screen where you name a session's steps and put each ticked concept into one. This is a drawing for approval before anything is built. Every concept and every step name on it is taken from Pivot — the deck you assembled by hand — so the page can be held up against your own document rather than against a description of it.

Drawn 2026-09-20, on your ruling of the same morning. Five decisions are asked at the end, one at a time.

The rule this screen exists to carry out — your ruling, 2026-09-20

The advisor names the steps himself. He ticks concepts, then names each step and drags ticked concepts into it — and a step holding nothing still prints on the agenda.

Pivot's step 5, Do It & Review It, has no slides behind it at all. No rule reading the tick list could ever produce it, which is why the app can never work the running order out for itself. That single page is the whole reason this screen exists.

What the plan does today — read from the running code, 2026-09-20

The app can only make one step, plus a closing one. And because it has no names of its own to give them, it borrows the names of our own screens — so the agenda a client reads in the room currently says:

  • “Run the frameworks” — 14 concepts
  • “Objectives and actions” — 2 concepts

Those are the labels on our wizard, printed onto your client's document. A client is being shown the app's own internal steps and told they are the plan.

Three things to notice

  • Porter's is one concept and two cards. It appears in step 1 for observations and step 2 for responses, and both write into the same table — which is how your Pivot runs it. The two cards already exist in the app today; this screen only decides where each one goes.
  • Step 5 holds nothing and still has a card. Deleting the empty card is how you remove a step; leaving it is how you keep it on the agenda.
  • Nothing is lost by getting it wrong. A concept dragged out of a step goes back to the left-hand list; what has already been typed into its table is untouched, because the answers are stored against the concept, never against the step.

The decisions

Each is one recommendation and one question a yes or no answers. They will be asked one at a time in chat; they are recorded here so the ruling and what was proposed both survive.

Decision 1 — where the step builder sits

It has to come after ticking and before running, because the order it sets is the order the session is run in.

Recommendation: a new stage 2, between Scope and Run the frameworks — so the rail becomes five stages, as drawn. Running the frameworks then follows the steps in the order you built them.

Does the step builder become its own stage between Scope and Run?

Decision 2 — what the screen looks like when it opens

Either every ticked concept starts in the left-hand list with no steps at all, or the app opens with one step already holding everything, which you then split up.

Recommendation: open with everything in the left-hand list and one empty step named “Step 1”. Starting from a single full step invites you to accept it and move on, which is today's behaviour wearing a new screen. Starting empty makes the naming the thing you do.

Does the screen open with the concepts unplaced, rather than pre-filled into one step?

Decision 3 — whether a session can be run with concepts still unplaced

An advisor may tick fourteen and place eleven, then move on.

Recommendation: let him move on, and say so plainly on the screen — the unplaced ones are simply not in the session, and the button reads “Run 11 of 14” rather than being greyed out. Blocking it would strand an advisor mid-meeting over a concept he decided against; a count he can read is a better guard than a locked button.

May a session run with some ticked concepts left unplaced, with the count shown?

Decision 4 — whether the objectives and actions stay a step of their own

Today the app adds a closing block called “Objectives and actions” after everything else. Pivot has no such step — its step 5 is yours and it is named Do It & Review It.

Recommendation: keep the closing block, but stop it naming itself. It becomes the last step on the board like any other, with a name you can change and concepts you can drag into it — so an advisor who wants Pivot's wording simply types it.

Does the closing block become an ordinary, renameable step on the board?

Decision 5 — whether step names are free text

Your five names are sentences of your own. Nothing in the decks supplies them.

Recommendation: free text, and nothing offered. No suggested list, and nothing written by the AI. A step name is the argument of the session, it is the first thing a client reads, and it is exactly the kind of wording this project does not let us invent.

Are step names free text only, with nothing suggested?

What this drawing does not settle

Where the steps are stored

The session already keeps a scope on strategy_sessions. The steps would most likely sit beside it, but no statement in that store has ever run against a real database, and nothing here asks you to approve a storage shape.

How a step reorders on a phone

Dragging is drawn here as it works on a laptop, which is where a planning session is actually run. A touch equivalent is a build detail, not a decision for you.

And one word that must not be reused

The page already uses step for its own four screens — Scope, Run, Objectives, Plan. The session's steps are a different thing entirely. Whatever these are called in the code, the two must never share a name, or a later session will wire one to the other.
