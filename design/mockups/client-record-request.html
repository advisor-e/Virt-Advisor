A transcript is not corrected, and this is the reason. It records what was said in a room, not a claim about the world. If a word was misheard, or your client says the meaning was different, their statement is attached to the meeting and travels with it from then on. That is exactly what the law asks for where a record cannot simply be rewritten — and it keeps the original honest.

Attach this statement Cancel

Recorded as the client's own words, dated, and shown beside that moment in the transcript from now on. It is never edited into the transcript itself, and the advisor is told it has been attached.

Why this is an attached statement and not an edit. IPP7 asks you to correct information where you can, and — where you decline — to attach the individual's own statement so that anyone reading the record afterwards sees it. A transcript is the second case almost every time: it is evidence of a conversation, and quietly rewriting what somebody said would destroy the only thing it is good for. The client's objection is preserved and so is the original. Both halves matter.

It also protects the advisor. Every coaching finding quotes the transcript verbatim. If the transcript could be edited after the fact, every finding built on it would silently stop matching its evidence.

What this drawing deliberately does NOT do
  • No client login, and no client-facing screen of any kind. Requests arrive by email or in person and a person at the firm records them.
  • Nothing is emailed by the application. The firm downloads a file and sends it themselves — P7's rule for the Meeting Summary, applied unchanged.
  • No automatic redaction of third parties. Deciding whether an employee's name should come out is a judgement about a real situation. The screen warns; it does not act.
  • No AI anywhere in this feature. Nothing here is generated, summarised or classified by a model. It moves text a firm already holds.
  • Nothing above the firm. No count of requests reaches a group, a brand or the platform — not even an anonymous one.
  • It does not answer IPP3A. That is the other open finding — notifying a third party your client discussed — and no software can. It is the firm's position, published on the Compliance page.
The questions — one at a time, please
Nine questions, and question 1 is the one everything else hangs off. I have drawn my recommendation throughout so there is something concrete to disagree with. Nothing is built and nothing will be until each of these has your word.

1 · Which documents is a client entitled to?

You said "meeting notes", and three different things carry that meaning. The transcript is the record of the conversation. The Meeting Summary is written for the client and already sent to them. My Coaching Notes is about the advisor — how they handled the meeting.

Recommendation: the transcript and the Meeting Summary. Not My Coaching Notes.

The coaching notes are the advisor's own information, generated for them, and P2 says they are shared upward only by the advisor's own act — handing them to a client is a bigger breach of that than handing them to a manager. ⚠ The honest counter-argument, stated rather than buried: those notes quote the client verbatim throughout, and a regulator could hold that the quoted parts are the client's information whoever the report is about. If you would rather be safe, the alternative is to provide the quotations from the coaching notes without the observations around them. It is more work and it is defensible either way.

2 · Who at the firm may serve a request?

Recommendation: the firm manager, and any advisor for meetings they recorded themselves.

Advisor-only would fail the moment somebody leaves, changes team or is on leave — and a deadline does not pause. The firm manager is already the person who answers for the firm's obligations on the Compliance page, so this sits with the same person.

3 · Does the advisor have to agree before their meeting is handed over?

Recommendation: no. They are told, never asked.

A person's right to their own information cannot wait on a colleague's approval, and an advisor who has left the firm could block it forever. Screen E is how they find out.

4 · Correction — an attached statement, never an edit to the transcript?

Recommendation: attached statement only.

A transcript records what was said, not what is true, and editing it would break every coaching finding that quotes it. The client's objection is preserved in their own words beside the moment they dispute. This is what the law asks for where a record cannot be rewritten.

5 · Should a client be able to have a meeting deleted before its retention date?

Recommendation: yes.

In New Zealand this is a courtesy rather than a right. In the UK and EU it is a hard right with a one-month deadline, and the assessment says so. Building it once, now, is cheaper than discovering it at the point of selling into a second market — and a firm that can honour the request will look considerably better than one that explains why it cannot.

6 · What deadline should the clock show?

Recommendation: 20 working days by default, and the firm can change it.

Twenty working days is New Zealand's statutory maximum. The UK and EU work to one calendar month, which is usually shorter. The retention period already cascades from you down to each firm; the deadline would follow exactly the same path, so a firm in another country can be given the right figure without new machinery.

7 · When the transcript has already expired, what does the screen say?

Recommendation: name the date it was destroyed and the promise it was kept under — never "nothing found".

Drawn on the fourth row of Screen B. "No records" reads as though the meeting never happened and invites somebody to go looking for a backup. "Destroyed on 11 May 2026, as promised" is the same fact told truthfully, and it is the strongest thing a firm can say to a client who asked. It is the rule already ruled for the follow-through screen, applied here.

8 · Third parties named in a transcript — warn, or remove?

Recommendation: warn clearly, and remove nothing automatically.

Your client may have named an employee, a family member or a customer. Whether that should come out before release depends on facts only the firm knows. Software guessing at it would be worse than useless: it would miss some, cut things it should not, and give the firm a false sense that the problem was handled.

9 · The wording on the tab and the three buttons.

Provisional throughout and yours to set. The tab reads "Client Requests". The buttons read "Prepare the copy", "Attach this statement" and "Delete permanently". The clock reads "4 working days left".

Recommendation: keep them, with one flag — "Client Requests" is broad enough that a later feature may want the same tab.

I have deliberately avoided "Subject Access Request", which is the legal term of art. Nobody in a small firm says it, and a tab nobody understands is a tab nobody opens.
