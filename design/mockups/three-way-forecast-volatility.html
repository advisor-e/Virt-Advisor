Wording and behaviour

  1. ✓ The block COMPARES the forecast to the history — RULED, and drawn above. A history-only block would have been an accurate copy of a report the advisor can already open in one click, and a second copy of a live screen is how two screens start disagreeing. The comparison exists only here, because only this screen holds a forecast.
  2. ✓ The heading — RULED: Your forecast against the normal range. The month count sits in the line beneath it. Chosen over “Your forecast against the last twelve months”, which was the first drawing's wording, because two by-month exports make the read 24 months and a heading naming twelve would then be wrong on screen. The count moves to the sub-line, where it can say what was actually measured.
  3. ✓ The dial — RULED IN. 🔴 Against the recommendation. The recommendation was the number alone, on density grounds: step 3 is the longest of the four screens and the dial adds roughly 210px for a figure the block already prints. Mike ruled the picture travels with the number. One consequence was recorded rather than discovered later: the dial's own green/amber/red is about the HISTORY's variability, and the two bands below are about the FORECAST. Those are different judgements and the same three colours now carry both, so the dial sits in its own panel with a line saying in terms that it measures the last twelve months and not the forecast.
  4. ✓ A WARNING BAND, not a plain statement — RULED. 🔴 Against the recommendation. The recommendation was two plain sentences and no banner, on the grounds that the Volatility Report's own coach panel says the arithmetic shows where a month sits and the advisor judges why. Mike ruled a band, like the negative-stock one on step 4. On reflection the ruling is the better one and the reason is in his own framing of this feature: a three-way forecast is read by a lender, and a quiet observational note is the wrong register for a document somebody else interrogates. The band reuses the component step 4 already has, so it is no new work. The ordinary observation — three months simply repeating last year's seasonality — stays plain text, because it is not a warning.
  5. ✓ No window switch — RULED. The block measures every complete month the exports gave, up to 24, and states the count. The 12 / 18 / 24 control stays on the full Volatility Report, where a window is meant to be changed. Here it would have been a fifth control on the densest screen, and nothing is hidden because the count is on the page.
  6. ✓ Step 3 only, for now — RULED. The read does not appear on step 4 with the finished forecast. It does its job where the sales figures are typed, which is the only moment its answer can change anything. Whether it joins the printed output is a decision about the funding pack as a whole, and it belongs with item 4.62 rather than being taken twice.
  7. ✓ TWO band levels — RULED. 🔴 Against the recommendation. Amber beyond the second deviation, red beyond the third. The recommendation was one level at the third, for one threshold and one meaning. Mike ruled two. Note what was NOT chosen and why it matters: a band at the FIRST deviation would be permanently lit — four of the twelve forecast months above sit outside it, and three of those the advisor never touched. On this client both ruled levels happen to fire on January alone; they diverge on other clients' figures.
  8. ✓ The chart's dots follow the bands — RULED. Amber = beyond the second, red = beyond the third. Without this, amber would have meant “outside the first deviation” on the dots and “beyond the second” on the band — the same colour carrying two meanings inside one block. The full Volatility Report keeps its own scheme, so the two screens differ; that was the stated cost and it was accepted. ⚠ ONE ADDITION FOLLOWS FROM THIS RULING and it was not put to Mike, because it is what the ruling requires rather than a fresh choice: a month merely outside the first deviation now has no marker left, which would have lost the “three of these are just last year's seasonality” reading that the paragraph under the chart depends on. Those months are drawn as a HOLLOW dot in their own series colour — visible, but not a severity colour. If the preference is that they carry no marker at all, it is one line.
  9. ✓ The band wording — RULED: version A, which names the month and asks for the reason. Both bands are drawn above in their approved words. Chosen over a shorter, purely observational version. It follows the house style set by the negative-stock band on step 4 — state the fact, then point at the action — and its closing line ties to the purpose Mike gave this report: the majority of three-way forecasts support funding requests.

One build consequence, recorded now so it is not discovered half-way. The bands are fixed once the files are read, but the forecast months change as the advisor types, so the comparison has to re-run on every edit. It is a comparison against figures the backend has already returned, not a second model — but the four figures, the dial, the bands and the month verdicts must all come from volatilityModel.js through the intake route, never be recomputed in the Vue component. The Stack Constitution is explicit that calculation does not happen in the frontend, and two implementations of a standard deviation is exactly how a screen and a report start disagreeing.
