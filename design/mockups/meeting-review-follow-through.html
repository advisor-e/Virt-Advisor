Follow-through

What you agreed last time with this client, and whether it came up this time. One new block inside a report that already exists — nothing else on that screen changes.

Drawn 2026-09-07 · not approved · nothing here is built. The matching and the checking ARE built and tested (server/utils/meetingFollowThrough.js), and the block is already computed and stored on every coaching report. No screen renders it, which is why this page exists: a screen is drawn and approved before it is built.
Why this needed the client on the meeting record. A meeting used to record the firm, the advisor, the meeting type and the retention period — but not which client. Matching on the advisor and the meeting type alone would have checked one client's agreed actions against a different client's transcript, and the result would have read as a perfectly ordinary report: real actions, real quotes, real timestamps, all from somebody else's business. The client picker went onto the pre-start screen on 2026-09-07, using wording already approved for the client register.

✓ Approved already ruled, and inherited

The report's name (My Coaching Notes), that it belongs to the advisor alone (P2), that every finding quotes the transcript or declares the thing absent (P4), and that a disagreement stays in the record (P5). This block inherits all four rather than restating them.

Where the block sits, its heading, the two state labels, and how it reads when there is no previous meeting or when the previous meeting's transcript has expired. Every word below is ours, and none of it is Mike's.

Three things this block does NOT do, each on purpose.
  • It never says an action was “not done”. A recording knows what was said in a room for an hour; it does not know what happened in the six months between. Not raised is the strongest true statement available, and that distinction is the whole reason the labels are worded as they are.
  • It quotes YOU, never the client. These are your coaching notes, and the question is whether the adviser returned to what was agreed. A client mentioning it unprompted is not you following it through — and the existing citation guard already drops a client's words, so this is inherited rather than newly built.
  • It carries no manager route. P2 holds unchanged: this is yours, and “Share with my manager” remains absent — the manager aggregate is anonymous by construction, so a named report still has nowhere to arrive.

When there is nothing to follow through

Two different reasons, and they must not read the same.
First meeting with this client ✎ Proposed
My Coaching Notes · Hale & Partners Discovery meeting · 7 September 2026

Since we last met

This is your first recorded meeting with this client. From your next one, what you agree today will appear here.
It says what will happen, not merely that nothing did. An advisor meeting this on their first recording should read it as the beginning of something rather than as a feature that failed to load.

The same panel covers a meeting recorded without choosing a client, with one sentence changed — “This meeting was not recorded against a client, so there is nothing to compare it with.” That is honest, and it is also a nudge: choosing the client next time is what turns this on.
The previous meeting's transcript has expired ✎ Proposed
My Coaching Notes · Whitfield & Co End of year meeting · 7 September 2026

Since we last met

Your last meeting with this client was on 2 January 2025, and its transcript has since been deleted. Your firm keeps transcripts for 18 months, which is what your client was told, so what was agreed that day is no longer held anywhere.
🔴 This is the panel that matters most, and it is why this page has three screens rather than one. Without it, an expired meeting renders exactly like a meeting where nothing was agreed — and an advisor would read “no actions” as a fact about their client rather than a fact about the retention clock.

It states the promise as the reason. The deletion is not a fault to apologise for; it is the firm's own retention period doing exactly what the client was told it would do. The figure is the firm's current one, rendered, never a hardcoded 18.

The consequence, stated so nobody meets it as a surprise: follow-through can only ever reach back as far as a firm's retention dial allows. A firm on six months has a six-month memory, and this panel is where that becomes visible.
3

What is being asked

Four, each with a recommendation and the argument against it.
  • 1 · Where it sits. Recommended: inside My Coaching Notes, above the observation points — an advisor reads what was outstanding before reading how today went, which is the order the conversation itself had. The argument against: it pushes the observation points, the report's main event, further down the page.
  • 2 · The heading. Recommended: “Since we last met”. Rejected: “Follow-through”, which is our internal word for it and reads like a score; and “Last meeting's actions”, which is accurate and flat.
  • 3 · The two state labels. Recommended: “You raised it” and “Not raised”. Rejected: “Done” / “Not done”, which the software cannot know and which would tell an advisor something untrue about their client; and “Found” / “Not found”, which the observation points below already use for a different question.
  • 4 · Whether the expired panel names the retention period. Recommended: yes — it turns a deletion into the firm's own policy visibly working. The argument against: it puts a data-retention sentence into a coaching report, which is not what an advisor opened it for.
