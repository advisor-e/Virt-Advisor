Three quick calculators that answer a question in ten seconds and feed nothing. Not a second business, and not a port — Mike, 2026-09-14: “its just a way that people can quickly calculate between salary and hrly rate. its not supposed to reflect the entire business, modify it as required.”

Nothing here is built

This is a drawing for approval. The five steps, the report and the Model Library card are built and live; this tab is not. The figures are sheet 1’s own — its typed inputs and its cached results — so they are the spreadsheet’s numbers, not ours. A golden test is what will later pin them.

Two of its results are already reproduced by hand from the stored formulas and are marked ✓ where they appear. That is the standard the build will be held to.

Four things to settle before it is built

Question 1 — does the blended rate survive?

Row 25 is the one genuinely clever thing on that sheet: given a rate per job type and how a person’s week is split across job types, it returns a single blended charge-out rate — $323.75 for the Director, $130.75 for the Junior Tech. It needs the job-type rates and the time mix, but it does not need headcounts or a firm.

Recommendation: keep it, for one person at a time. “This person does 35% Job Type 1 at $375 and 40% Job Type 5 at $275 — what are they worth an hour?” is the same question as salary ⇄ hourly, asked the other way round, and it is the part an adviser cannot do in their head. Dropping it would lose the sheet’s only real calculation.

Question 2 — one tax layer or two?

The sheet runs two tax tables — one headed “State Taxes” holding the five NZ rates, and a second headed “Federal Taxes” holding what are plainly Australian ones ($18,200 tax-free, then 19%). Its own note says “scroll right to view combined (concurrent) State & Federal Taxes”.

⚠ The Federal table is computed and then used by nothing. It works out $24,991.75 and no cell reads it. The headline $42,165 comes entirely from the NZ table — $38,770 on the main income plus $3,395 on the spouse’s. So the sheet describes a two-layer country and calculates a one-layer one.

Recommendation: one layer, from the country picker. That is what the sheet actually does, it is what a New Zealand or UK adviser needs, and the two-layer case is a US/Australian arrangement the rest of the app has no concept of. Nothing is lost, because nothing was using it.

Question 2b — what does “spouse split” mean? It changes the headline

The sheet taxes the full $145,000 on one table and the $25,000 on a second copy of the same table, then adds them. It never subtracts the 25,000 from the 145,000. So it is taxing $170,000 of income from a $145,000 salary.

That is right if “spouse split” means the spouse separately earns $25,000. It is wrong if it means $25,000 of the $145,000 is allocated to the spouse — in which case the main earner should be taxed on $120,000 and the correct total is $33,915, not $42,165. The label reads like the second; the arithmetic does the first.

Recommendation: label it “Spouse’s own income” and keep the arithmetic. It is the reading the formulas support, and it is the more useful calculation — a household’s combined tax. ⚠ But this is a question about what you meant, not a defect I should decide. If you meant income splitting, the figure moves by $8,250 and I will change the maths instead of the label.

Question 3 — where do the bands come from, given the fifth Tax Rates figure is not built?

Decision 5 is already ruled: income tax bands become a fifth figure in Tax Rates, NZ badged app default, never withheld. That work is outstanding. Today the store holds four figures — companyTax, gst, filing, basis.

Recommendation: build the tab against Tax Rates from the start, and build the fifth figure first. The alternative is a tab holding its own private band table that has to be unpicked weeks later, and a firm that has corrected its bands in Tax Rates seeing stale ones here. It makes this job bigger by one step and leaves nothing to undo.

Question 4 — the superannuation label, which is the only thing on this tab that could mislead

The bonus calculator deducts 7% superannuation from the gross, which treats it as the employee’s contribution. The Seasonal Inputs sheet separately carries a 3% “Employer Retirement Contribution”, which is the firm’s cost on top of pay. Both are the adviser’s own typed fields, so neither is wrong — but one screen would hold two retirement percentages meaning opposite things, and an adviser typing 3% here would get a quietly wrong bonus.

Recommendation: label it “Employee superannuation %” on this tab and leave the model’s field as it is. Two words, and the ambiguity is gone. The wording is yours — this is a suggestion, not a decision.

Where it lives, and what it does not do

A second tab on /wages-review, beside “The model”. Not a Model Library card of its own: it is a calculator, not a model, and a card promises a report.

It saves nothing and feeds nothing. Sheet 1 is referenced 0 times by any other sheet and references 0 itself — proven by counting in both directions — so the tab cannot move a figure on the five steps or the report. That isolation is the reason it is safe to build now.

No personal data. Salaries and rates, never a named person. The staff register is the gated screen and is nothing to do with this tab.
