Decision 5 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-14 — no separate tab

His words: “does it need a separate tab? couldn’t it just import the tax data into a hidden section and apply across the model as needed?” — and on the answer below, the tab is dropped and the two converters move to step 1.

He is right about the data, and it already works that way. The income tax bands live in Firm Manager → Tax Rates, resolve by country through the existing cascade, and any screen can read them. No tab is needed for that and none ever was.

🔴 But nothing in the wages model needs them, and checking settled it: the model computes no tax anywhere. Labour margin is what the team bills minus what it costs. Income tax is the employee’s, taken out of their gross pay — and gross pay is already the wage cost. Deducting it again would count it twice. So “apply across the model as needed” has no consumer to apply to.

That reframed the four calculators, and two of them were in the wrong place. Salary ⇄ hourly and the blended rate exist to fill in a box on step 1 — the pay rate and the charge-out rate. Behind a tab, an adviser has to leave the flow to find them, which is how a helper goes unused. The other two serve no step of this model at all.

Question 1 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-14 — the blended rate stays, one person at a time

Row 25 survives as its own calculator on this tab. One person, no headcounts, no roles, no firm — which is what keeps it inside his “not supposed to reflect the entire business”.

It is the one genuinely clever thing on that sheet: given a rate per kind of work and how a person’s week splits across them, it returns a single blended charge-out rate. “This person does 35% advisory at $375 and 40% supervision at $275 — what are they worth an hour?” is the same question as salary ⇄ hourly asked the other way round, and it is the part an adviser cannot do in their head. Dropping it would have left three arithmetic conversions.

⚠ The time-mix check comes with it. The workbook carries a “Balance Time Remaining” row, and it is what makes the calculation trustworthy: a mix adding to 80% quietly returns a rate a fifth too low, and nothing else on the screen would look wrong.

Question 2 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-14 — one layer, and it comes from Tax Rates

His words: “the taxes need to link to the tax tables elsewhere in the firm manager hub — select which country you’re in and the taxes apply, as they should.”

This tab holds no tax table of its own. Pick the country and the bands are whatever that country’s approved table says, resolved through the same cascade as the company tax rate, the GST rate, the filing cycle and the accounting basis. Correct a band in Firm Manager → Tax Rates and it is corrected here the same moment — there is no second copy to go stale, and no adviser reading one figure while their manager has approved another.

It also settles the sheet’s two-table question by making it moot. Sheet 1 ran a “State Taxes” column holding New Zealand’s rates and a “Federal Taxes” column holding Australian ones — and the second was computed and read by nothing, working out $24,991.75 that no cell used. The $42,165 came entirely from the first. The sheet described a two-layer country and calculated a one-layer one; the country table does the same thing honestly.

✅ Already built, ahead of this tab. Income tax bands landed as the fifth Tax Rates figure on 2026-09-14 — cascading, approve-before-use, and validated against a gap, an overlap, an implicit tax-free hole and a closed top band. A country nobody has loaded falls back to the app’s own bands badged as defaults, never withheld: Mike’s ruling of 2026-09-08, “Never block the advisor.”

Question 2b — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-14 — “own or allocated income”

His words: “own income — or more importantly, own or allocated income.” The field is labelled “Spouse’s own or allocated income”, and the arithmetic is kept exactly as the workbook has it.

Those two words settle what looked like a defect. The sheet taxes the full $145,000 on one table and the $25,000 on a second copy, and never subtracts — so it appeared to tax $170,000 of income from a $145,000 salary. Under his ruling it does not: both figures are each person’s own taxable income, typed independently, because any allocation has already happened before either is entered. There is nothing to subtract.

“Allocated” is the half that matters. It covers income the spouse never earned but is taxed on — a trust distribution, a partnership share, a salary allocated from the business — which is precisely the case an adviser meets and which “own income” alone would have excluded.

What this closes: the alternative reading would have made the correct answer $33,915 instead of $42,165, a difference of $8,250. The figure stands at $42,165 and the golden test will pin it.

Question 3 — where do the bands come from, given the fifth Tax Rates figure is not built?

Decision 5 is already ruled: income tax bands become a fifth figure in Tax Rates, NZ badged app default, never withheld. That work is outstanding. Today the store holds four figures — companyTax, gst, filing, basis.

Recommendation: build the tab against Tax Rates from the start, and build the fifth figure first. The alternative is a tab holding its own private band table that has to be unpicked weeks later, and a firm that has corrected its bands in Tax Rates seeing stale ones here. It makes this job bigger by one step and leaves nothing to undo.

Question 4 — RULED BY MIKE 2026-09-14 — “Employee superannuation %”

The field on this tab reads “Employee superannuation %”. The model’s own Employer Retirement Contribution is untouched.

What it prevents. The bonus calculator deducts its 7% from the gross, which only makes sense as the employee’s contribution. The Seasonal Inputs sheet separately carries 3% as the firm’s cost on top of pay. Both are the adviser’s own typed fields, so neither is wrong — but one model would hold two retirement percentages meaning opposite things, and an adviser typing the 3% they know into this box would get a quietly wrong bonus with nothing on screen to say so.

Two words, and the ambiguity is gone.

Where it lives, and what it does not do

A second tab on /wages-review, beside “The model”. Not a Model Library card of its own: it is a calculator, not a model, and a card promises a report.

It saves nothing and feeds nothing. Sheet 1 is referenced 0 times by any other sheet and references 0 itself — proven by counting in both directions — so the tab cannot move a figure on the five steps or the report. That isolation is the reason it is safe to build now.

No personal data. Salaries and rates, never a named person. The staff register is the gated screen and is nothing to do with this tab.
