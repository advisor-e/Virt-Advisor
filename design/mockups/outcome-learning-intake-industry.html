Questions for Mike — each carries one recommendation; put one at a time

1. Suggest from the whole vocabulary, or from an industry-only list? Ruled 2026-09-11: the whole vocabulary, as recommended

Recommended: the whole vocabulary, filtered by what is typed. It is exactly the list the pool accepts, so a chip can never offer a word the pool would then drop; the typed prefix removes most of the noise ("caf" offers only "cafe"); and there is no second list for anyone to maintain. The cost is Screen A: "plugins" and "plus" beside "plumber".

Against: the 89 Revenue & Feasibility models are "named by industry" and would give a cleaner list — but that list holds Assumptions, Break-Even and Quick Position too, so it is not an industry list either, and a word offered from it that the pool then rejected would be worse than a noisy chip.

2. Three letters before anything is offered, and eight chips at most? Ruled 2026-09-11: yes to both, as recommended

Recommended: yes to both. One or two letters would offer dozens of words for "pl"; three narrows it to a row. Eight fits one line under the box at the widths the chat runs at. Both are numbers, so they are asked rather than assumed.

3. A click replaces the whole answer, not just the word being typed? Ruled 2026-09-11: the whole answer, as recommended

Recommended: the whole answer — Screen B's reasoning. The pool keeps a whole answer that equals one word; a chip that only patched the last word would let "car yard plumber" look chosen and never pool.

Against: an advisor who has typed a longer description loses it on the click. They can see it happen and retype; the alternative fails silently.

4. The label reads "Pick one, or keep typing:" ? Ruled 2026-09-11: approved as written

Recommended: yes. It says the chips are optional in six words. Wording is Mike's call and is asked, never assumed.
