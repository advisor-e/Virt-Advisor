Because the accounts follow the tax rates, Investment Boost is what Inland Revenue describes and nothing more — 20% expensed, 80% capitalised and depreciated as normal. One rate throughout.

Three screens in one file on purpose, against this feature's own habit: it is one rule approved on the manager's table, applied on the advisor's purchase list, and reported on its own line in the printed forecast. Apart, the only things worth checking would be invisible.

2 · Asset purchases become a dated list

🔴 THE BIGGEST CHANGE ON THIS PAGE, AND IT IS MIKE'S: "the boost requires specific time periods of purchase etc so the asset data needs to include the date it was purchased". The forecast records asset buying as twelve monthly totals today — a figure for June, a figure for July — with no date anywhere. Investment Boost starts on 22 May 2025, part-way through a month, so a month cannot answer the eligibility question at the boundary, and a claim with no purchase date behind it cannot be evidenced afterwards.

Ruling 4: purchases become a dated list per category and the twelve monthly totals are derived from it. The twelve boxes do not survive alongside it — one way to enter a purchase, not two.

🔴 THE ENGINE IS NOT TOUCHED. The monthly series it works to is computed from this list, so its input shape is unchanged and the 3,385-cell golden set that pins Mike's workbook stays valid. A forecast with an empty list produces exactly what it produces today. The screen changes; the arithmetic does not.

What the app decides and what it does not. It works out the split — 20% and 80% — and it refuses a date before the rule starts, because the advisor cannot see that date from this screen. 3 · Its own line in the printed forecast

The line takes its name from the approved rule itself — Investment Boost here — so a country whose scheme is called something else carries its own name rather than New Zealand's.

5 · What this deliberately does NOT do
  • It never touches the opening asset values. Investment Boost is for assets bought, so it acts only on purchases made during the forecast — never on what the business already owned.
  • It does not decide what qualifies. The advisor states the amount; the rule's own words are on the manager's card.
  • It introduces no second depreciation rate and no deferred tax. Mike's ruling of 2026-09-09 stands untouched: one rate, charged as an expense, tax computed on the profit after it.
  • It does not appear at all for a country with no approved rule. Not greyed out — absent, so an Australian client is never offered a New Zealand scheme.
  • It is not switched on by the app. Like every rate here, the rule reaches a forecast only after a firm manager has approved it.
  • It does not change the forecast engine. The monthly totals are derived from the purchase list, so the golden set that pins the workbook is untouched.

This absorbs item 4.77's asset-model change. That item asks for "assets carrying a rate or deduction that changes at a date", which is exactly the dated purchase list in §2. Neither item has been closed or merged on the live list — that is Mike's to decide, and it is recorded here rather than done quietly.
