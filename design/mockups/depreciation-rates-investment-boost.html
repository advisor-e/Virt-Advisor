🔴 A CORRECTION THIS DRAWING CARRIES. Earlier the same day he was told Investment Boost contradicted his ruling that the forecast holds one depreciation rate. It does not. That ruling is exactly what makes it simple: because the accounts follow the tax rates, Investment Boost is what Inland Revenue describes and nothing more — 20% expensed, 80% capitalised and depreciated as normal. One rate throughout. No second schedule, no deferred tax, and the asset schedule's arithmetic is untouched — only the figure entering it changes.

Both halves are in this one file on purpose, because they are one rule in two places: it is approved on the manager's table, and it switches on a field in the advisor's forecast. Apart, the only thing worth checking would be invisible.

It is read from the tax authority's own material, it shows its source, and nothing uses it until the firm manager approves it — the same gate as the rates themselves. A country whose documents contain no such scheme simply has no rule, and the advisor's field never appears.

4 · The third question, and what this deliberately does not do
QUESTION 3 — a purchase dated before the rule starts. A forecast can begin before 22 May 2025. Drawn so the qualifying field accepts nothing in a month before the rule's start date and says why on the row. Recommended over letting the figure through: a boost claimed on a purchase that predates the scheme is a wrong tax figure in a document a lender reads, and the advisor cannot see the start date from the forecast screen.
  • It never touches the opening asset values. Investment Boost is for assets bought, so it acts only on purchases made during the forecast — never on what the business already owned.
  • It does not decide what qualifies. The advisor states the amount. The screen shows the rule's own words for what does and does not qualify, and stops there — judging whether a particular tractor is "new to New Zealand" is the accountant's job, not the app's.
  • It introduces no second depreciation rate and no deferred tax. Mike's ruling of 2026-09-09 stands untouched: one rate, charged as an expense, tax computed on the profit after it.
  • It does not appear at all for a country with no approved rule. Not greyed out — absent, so an Australian client is never offered a New Zealand scheme.
  • It is not switched on by the app. Like every rate here, the rule reaches a forecast only after a firm manager has approved it.
