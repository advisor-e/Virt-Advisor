Decision 3 of 3 — which way is "good" Ruled by Mike 2026-09-12

✅ RULED AS RECOMMENDED. The arithmetic stays actual minus budget, exactly as the workbook has it, and the colour carries the meaning with a plain Better or Worse beside it. The recommendation as it was put to him is left below.

The same sign means opposite things on the two halves of this table. Spending 1,200 more than budget is bad; earning 1,200 more than budget is good. Your spreadsheet computes actual − budget for both and leaves the reader to work it out.

Recommended, and drawn above: keep the arithmetic exactly as your spreadsheet has it — always actual minus budget — and colour by what it means, not by its sign, with a plain word beside it: Better or Worse. So on the money-out lines a green −18,000 reads "spent 18,000 less than planned".

The alternative is flipping the sign on expenses so green is always positive. That makes the colour easy and the number wrong: it would no longer match the figure in your own Cashflow Variances sheet, and someone checking one against the other would find them disagreeing.

What is NOT on this page, and why

  • The GST movements rows. The model computes them and they drive the bank balance, but they are plumbing, not a finding. They would be four more rows of arithmetic an owner does not read. Say if you want them shown.
  • A "print" button. Every other model in this section is a screen an advisor drives, not a document a client takes away — printing belongs to the Business Performance Report. This model could feed an optional page there later; that is a separate decision and not this build.
  • 🔴 Anything read from a file — and the accounts readers we already have CANNOT do it. Half of this is easy: a budget is a forecast, so there is nothing to import on the budget side at all. The actuals are the tempting half, and the trap. This is a CASH budget; a Profit and Loss is accrual. A P&L counts a sale the day it is invoiced and this model counts it the day the money reaches the bank — different by the whole debtor movement. Three of these lines (Drawings, Principal loan repayments, Plant & equipment) are not on a P&L at all, being balance-sheet movements, and a P&L carries depreciation, which must never appear in a cash budget because no cash moves. Wiring the existing readers in would put a cash budget column beside an accrual actual column, looking identical — the same not-like-for-like fault this model was built to correct. The right file is a cash-basis export (Xero's Cash Summary); we have never seen one, so a reader for it would be a guess. Mike raised this himself on 2026-09-12 — "should we have built the ability to load a balance sheet and p&L to help with the 'fast data load'" — was shown the above, and ruled leave it for now.
