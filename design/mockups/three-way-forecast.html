NOTE What a file can and cannot tell us

An accounting export records what has happened. A forecast is about what will. So the files fill in where the business stands today and what it currently spends — and the forecast itself, the sales, the buying, the terms, stays your judgement. Every figure on the next screens says which it is.

COACH What this forecast is saying

The business is trading at a loss and running on the overdraft. Cash starts 226,000 overdrawn and ends 401,000 overdrawn — it never recovers, and no month reaches a positive balance.

The gross margin is 24.5%, and overheads are running near 38,000 a month. On these sales the trade does not cover them: the gap is roughly 20,000 a month, which is the shape of the whole problem.

Before showing this to the client, fix the two months where stock goes negative — the forecast is buying too little to support the sales it assumes.

Wording and behaviour still needing Mike’s word

  1. The four headline figures. Drawn as Cash at year end, Lowest point, Result for the year, Gross margin. Are those the four an accountant wants first? The banner takes exactly four.
  2. The plain-English row labels. The cash-flow table says Money in, Money out, Movement, Cash at month end; working capital says Owed to you, Stock on hand, You owe. Your workbook calls them Receipts, Less Payments, Net Movement, Closing Balance, Accounts Receivable, Inventory, Accounts Payable. Plain English for the client, or your own accounting terms?
  3. “Starting point” — the green tag on figures seeded from last year, as against from file and entered. Is that the right word for it?
  4. The negative-stock warning. Drawn as a red band plus red cells. Is flagging it right, or should the forecast simply show the figures and leave the reading to the advisor?
  5. The balance-check card. It shows the opening 164,000 gap plainly. Should an out-of-balance opening position block the forecast, or only warn as drawn?
  6. Step 3’s heading — Set the assumptions. And the four step names as a set.
  7. The supported-software line. Drawn as “Reports exported from Xero, QuickBooks Online and MYOB can be read. Xero is confirmed against real exports; QuickBooks Online and MYOB are supported from their published layout, so check the figures on the next step.” It appears on every intake screen, not just this one. Is naming the unconfirmed two the right call, or too much detail for an advisor?
  8. Month stepping — RULED, and the drawing was already right. The months are shown as Apr…Mar on the first of each. Your workbook steps 31 days at a time, so its month twelve began 8 March and three years ran three weeks adrift. Ruled 2026-09-02 (“obviously, it needs to be per calendar month”) and built — nothing to decide here.
