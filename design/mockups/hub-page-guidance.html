How to use this page

Every time an advisor finishes a case, they tick how each recommended template went. Firms that share send those ticks here with no names attached. This page is where you read them and decide whether the engine should act on them.

  1. Each row is one template in one kind of situation. Delivered is how often it was used; Didn't land is how often the advisor said it fell flat. The hold-back is how much the engine proposes to recommend it less readily — never removed, and never over what an advisor said about their own client today.
  2. Accept makes the hold-back live at every sharing firm within a minute. Hold parks it. Reject refuses it and asks you why. A row below the floor cannot be accepted yet: it says how many more firms and cases it needs.
  3. The top of the table is where to look. A template that keeps being delivered and keeps not landing is the platform telling you where the content or the logic tables need attention.
  4. Press Run the benches after you decide. The bottom card then says whether your decisions made recommendations better, before and after.

How to use this page

A firm manager who disagrees with one of your defaults can change it in their own Logic Lab and push that change back up. This page rolls those pushed changes up to you, grouped where several firms made the same one.

  1. Read the feed first, not the counts. Each pushed edit shows the sentence the manager probed, what the engine did with it, the template they expected, and the change they chose. That is what a firm was trying to achieve, and what they had to do to get there.
  2. One firm is a preference. Several firms making the same change is a default of yours that needed fixing. The grouping does this arithmetic for you; the label on each group says which.
  3. Act at the top. When a group says platform gap, change the default once in the logic tables so the correction cascades to everyone and the workarounds stop.
  4. This page is about rules firms changed, not results clients got. Outcome Learning is the page for what actually landed.

The questions as they were put — kept beside the rulings

1 · Is the reading made on a button, or every time the page opens?

The client reports make their reading as part of producing the report. These pages recompute on every open, and a mentor may open Outcome Learning several times in a sitting.

Recommended: on the button, kept with its date and counts, and marked stale when the pool has changed. One call per press, none per open, and the mentor always knows which numbers a reading was made from. Against: a mentor who never presses it never sees it — the empty card's sentence exists to invite the press.

2 · Does the panel close for good, or reopen every visit?

Screen A shows it open with a Got it button.

Recommended: open on the first visit, closed after Got it, remembered on that browser; the title stays on the page so it can be opened again in one click. Against: a mentor on a new machine sees it open once more — which is the right outcome.

3 · May the reading name a template and say what to do with it?

The example on Screen A says the live hold-back on Break-Even is the right call and that its twin row should be held.

Recommended: yes — a reading that will not name anything is a horoscope. The caveat line under every reading says it is advice, the buttons are still the mentor's, and the backend refuses a reply that does not carry the three headings. Against: a mentor may take the advice uncritically; the honesty line and the counts beside every row are the counterweight, and the same trust is already extended to the client reports.

4 · Which tiers?

Outcome Learning is the mentor's alone by its own ruling. The Logic-Lab Report exists at the mentor and both middle tiers.

Recommended: the panel and the reading go wherever the page goes — mentor alone for Outcome Learning, all three tiers for the Logic-Lab Report, because it is one component reading the level below each viewer. Against: none found; building it for one tier of a three-tier page would be a stated judgement with no reason behind it.

What this drawing does not decide The exact prompt wording is a backend text on the AI Prompts tab, where every prompt already lives and can be changed without a build. The model and its cost are the platform's existing OpenAI route; one reading is one short call.
