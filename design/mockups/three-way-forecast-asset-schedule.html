31,500 is typed, and nothing checks it. The app holds six category totals and never an individual asset, so it cannot know what one van is carried at. The advisor reads the figure off their own Fixed Asset Schedule and types it in. A wrong book value is not a visible mistake: it moves the gain, which moves the tax, which moves retained earnings and the closing cash — and every one of those still balances. This is precisely the class of fault a person in UAT cannot see.

4 · The finding that shapes the whole design

The schedule does not tie to the balance sheet — in either real export

MYOB — Balance Sheet “Total Property, Plant & Equipment” 145,300.00
MYOB — Asset Register, eleven assets totalled 128,775.83   out by 16,524.17
QuickBooks — Balance Sheet “Total Fixed Assets” 145,300.00
QuickBooks — Fixed Asset Listing, eleven assets totalled 125,825.00   out by 19,475.00
This is normal, and it is why the schedule must never become the opening position. An asset register is a sub-ledger. It runs its own depreciation, it is reconciled to the balance sheet periodically rather than continuously, and it commonly omits fully-written-down or pooled items. Both of Mike’s exports show it, and the two schedules do not even agree with each other — identical cost of 224,500, book values 2,950.83 apart, because MYOB depreciates some assets on diminishing value and QuickBooks straight-line.

So the schedule is used for ONE job: looking up one asset’s book value when it is sold. The Balance Sheet stays the source of the opening asset position, exactly as it is today. Had this drawing let the schedule seed the six categories, it would have understated fixed assets by 16,524 and charged too little depreciation all year — and the forecast would still have balanced.

6 · Six questions, one recommendation each
Question 1 — the one that matters

Should the book value be depreciated to the month of the sale, or shown as the schedule’s year-end figure?

The schedule says what the Ford Ranger was worth at the last balance date: 31,500. If it is sold in March, three months of depreciation have been charged against it first, so the book value at the moment of sale is 30,712.50 — and the gain is 2,712.50, not 3,500.

Recommendation: depreciate it, and show the working under the figure. The engine already charges depreciation monthly on the category pool (threeWayForecastModel.js), so using the year-end figure would credit the business with an asset it has already partly written down — overstating the gain in every sale after January, by more the later it falls.
The argument against, recorded: the advisor sees a number that is not on the paper in front of them. That is why the working is printed under it rather than beside it in a tooltip — they must be able to get from 31,500 to 30,712.50 without asking anyone.
Question 2

When the schedule’s group does not match one of our six categories, does the app guess?

MYOB says Motor Vehicles; we call it Vehicles. MYOB says Plant & Machinery; we call it Plant and equipment. QuickBooks says 1500 Workshop Machinery & Tools, which matches nothing by name at all.

Recommendation: the app proposes, the advisor confirms. Pre-select the closest category, leave the dropdown fully editable, and mark it guessed until the advisor touches it. Anything it cannot place is left blank rather than dropped into Other.
The argument against: a badge nobody reads is decoration. The protection is that the category picks the depreciation rate, so a wrong guess is visible as a wrong depreciation charge — not silent.
Question 3

When the schedule does not tie to the balance sheet, does the app say so?

Neither of the two real exports ties — out by 16,524 and 19,475 (§4). So this is the ordinary case, not the exception.

Recommendation: say it once, quietly, and never block. One line under the schedule: the two totals and the difference. It changes no figure, because the balance sheet remains the opening position either way.
The argument against: a message on almost every upload is one advisers learn to skip. It earns its place because the alternative — saying nothing — leaves an advisor who does expect them to tie with no way to discover they don’t.
Question 4

Do we use MYOB’s per-asset depreciation rate and method?

MYOB gives a rate and a method for every asset. QuickBooks gives neither. Our engine depreciates the category pool at one rate.

Recommendation: no — read them, store them, use neither. Using a per-asset rate means a per-asset engine, which is a different forecast model and a much larger job than this item. Taking MYOB’s rate and not QuickBooks’ would also make the same business forecast differently depending on which package it happens to use.
The argument against: we hold a truer number and use a rougher one. Fairly stated — but the category rate is the advisor’s own setting on the same screen, and it is the one they can see and change.
Question 5

Can an advisor still sell something that is not on the schedule?

An asset bought and sold inside the same forecast year never appears on last year’s schedule.

Recommendation: yes — the last option in the chooser is “Something else — type it in”, which restores today’s two typed boxes for that row alone. The chooser must never be the only way in.
The argument against: two ways to fill one row is two things to test. Accepted: the alternative is an advisor who cannot record a real sale.
Question 6

Does selling an asset remove it from the schedule for the rest of the forecast?

If the Ford Ranger is sold in March, it should not be offerable again in September.

Recommendation: yes — an asset already used on a Sell row drops out of the chooser for every other row. Selling the same van twice is not a thing that can happen, and the forecast would balance perfectly while doing it.
The argument against: none found. Recorded as a question rather than assumed because it is a rule, and rules belong to Mike.
7 · What this does not solve
Named on the page rather than discovered later:
  • The schedule still does not become the opening position. The six category totals come from the Balance Sheet, as they do today — §4 is why.
  • Xero has not been checked. Xero is the one package with a long history of real exports here, and no Xero Fixed Asset Schedule has ever been read. Its shape is a third shape, and this drawing does not know it.
  • No screen shows the schedule after step 1. It is read, used for the chooser, and not displayed again. If an advisor wants to see the eleven assets, they open their own file.
  • Nothing reconciles the disposal back to the category. Selling an asset carried at 30,712.50 out of a pool the Balance Sheet says holds more is arithmetically fine and conceptually loose — the pool is the pool.
