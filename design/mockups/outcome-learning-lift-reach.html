Why a third column — question 3. The built honesty line already admits the outcome bench is in-sample. This column is the fix: the cut-off is the start of the latest month in the pool (spec assumption), the adjustments are recomputed from everything before it, only the ids the mentor has marked live are applied, and the score is taken on the latest month alone (research R7). The heading names the months so the mentor never has to work out which is which. When the test month holds fewer than 25 reviews the column shows the sentence in the small card and no figure (spec edge case). The fixed bench's "before" is always 100%, as built and ruled 2026-09-11.

The cap-breach line is spec FR-006 made visible: the count of cases where a pooled adjustment re-ordered a template against the advisor's own evidence. It is expected to read 0 on every run; a mentor who sees any other number is looking at a bug, and the line says so.

Open questions — four, one recommendation each, yes or no

1 · One "Adjustment" column with a direction pill — or two columns, "Lift" and "Hold-back"?

Drawn as one column: a signed number and a small green or amber word beside it. The alternative is two columns where every row has one empty cell.

Recommended: one column, as drawn. The ruling is one number per pairing, and one column says so; the pill makes the sign readable without a minus sign doing all the work. Against: "+2" and "−4" in one column can be misread as a scale rather than a direction, which is what the pill is there to prevent — and sorting by size then mixes lifts and hold-backs, so the column sorts by absolute size, biggest effect first.

2 · One reach tile, "Loop reach: 41 of 118" — or two, "Reviews recorded" and "Not yet reviewed"?

Drawn as one tile with the share and the read time on its sub-line. Two tiles would give the 77 unreviewed cases a box of their own.

Recommended: one tile, as drawn. The strip already has four tiles; five fit, six wrap on a laptop, and 41 of 118 says both numbers. Against: 77 is the number that should nag — the cases the loop cannot learn from — and a tile of its own would nag harder. It is one subtraction away.

3 · Is the three-sentence honesty line right?

"The outcome bench scores the adjustments on the same reviews that produced them, so it flatters them. The out-of-sample bench is trained on the earlier months and tested on the latest, so it cannot see its own answers. Read the fixed bench as a guard, and the out-of-sample figure as the honest number."

Recommended: approve as drawn. Each sentence names one bench and what to make of it; "the honest number" is the phrase a mentor will repeat to a buyer. Against: three sentences under three figures is a lot of small text; a shorter line would have to drop either what "in-sample" means or which figure to trust, and the built line already showed that dropping one leaves the reader guessing.

4 · Is the refusal wording on the firm's tab right?

"Sharing can't be switched on yet — the platform's outcome pool isn't configured. Ask Advisor-e to set it. Nothing has been changed." Shown in red under the Start sharing button.

Recommended: approve as drawn. It says what happened, whose job it is, and that the press did nothing — the three things a manager needs before walking away. Against: "outcome pool" is our term and a firm manager has not met it; but the amber line already on the page uses the same words, and naming the thing is what lets Advisor-e find it.

Which tiers get this, and why

The mentor page: mentor tier alone — the judgement is unchanged (spec FR-014 on 4.87, restated here as the hub-page rule requires). A lift is the same kind of thing as a hold-back: one platform-wide finding, one mentor decision. A global group manager or group manager would see the same rows, the same reach figure and the same three benches, and could take no different decision on any of them. Nothing here gives a lower tier a value of its own.

The firm tab: firm tier alone — unchanged. The reach pair is the firm's own two counts and nothing about anyone else; the refusal is shown to the person pressing the switch, who is the firm manager. No middle tier gets either screen. They contribute no reviews, receive no adjustment, and flip no switch; cascading becomes mandatory the day one of them has a real reason to hold a different value, and none has one today.
