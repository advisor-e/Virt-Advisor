1 · Why there was a problem at all

The feed already carried the firm id in its payload. No screen displayed it. So every manager opened Case Reviews to a stack of anonymous cards and could act on none of them — an alarm with no address.

ADVISOR-E-DESIGN-LOGIC.md §2, from Mike's own words: "The reports cascade up so we learn what is working, what isn't, who is failing so we can offer help."

Two rules had to be satisfied at once, and they pull against each other:

§2 — the purpose A manager must be able to see who needs help. Strip the origin and the report cannot do the only job it has.
Rule 7 / §4.1 Each level sees the level immediately below it, summarised — never a flat roster of the bottom of the tree.

A path satisfies both and stops them competing. Element 0 is the level immediately below the viewer — what the list is grouped by. The remainder is the address inside that group, which is what makes a group worth opening. Rule 7 limits what a list is grouped by; it does not blindfold a manager to their own firms.

4 · What is still hidden, and what "anonymised" protects

Shown Why it is not a disclosure
The firm Anonymisation here protects the client — the end business the adviser was advising. Naming a firm to the manager above it is not a disclosure: they are their firms. §4.4 records the same mistake being made four days earlier — applying an outside party's privacy boundary to the customer's own senior people.
The country / brand Carried in the reserved scope id itself. Nothing new is stored.
Still hidden Unchanged by this work
The adviser advisorId is stripped and stays stripped. A manager sees which firm to offer help to, never which individual to blame — help, not score (§2).
The client Summary and transcript are the anonymised copies the firm manager approved. Double opt-in is unchanged.

5 · Two limits, named rather than discovered later

The grouped view cannot be demonstrated No middle-tier login exists and no membership data has been supplied, so the deeper path is evidenced by tests against a seeded map, not a live screen. A weaker claim than a working demo, and recorded as one.
LIMIT 500 is applied before scoping Pre-existing. A tier's slice is taken from the 500 most recently shared cases platform-wide rather than from its own 500, so a very large platform would show a small group less than it has. It stops mattering when the mapping lands and the scope becomes an SQL IN clause.
One firm, two spellings Not caused here, but adjacent and worth naming: Adoption resolves real firm names through firmsDirectory, while the Logic-Lab Report still prints raw ids. Case Reviews uses the directory, so it does not become a third spelling — but the Logic-Lab Report is still the odd one out.
