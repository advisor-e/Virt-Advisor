FOUR RULINGS FROM MIKE, 2026-09-26, and the drawing shows each one. (1) The advisor enters the supplier's invoice in its own currency with an assumed exchange rate; each payment converts at that rate and becomes the stock's cost. (2) Freight and duty join the stock's cost and are charged as the stock sells, not in full in the month it lands. (3) Overseas sales are entered in the customer's currency with an assumed rate; an exchange gain or loss arises only on money not yet collected. (4) Both 10% allowances stop being a cost. Each becomes a “what if the rate moves” setting whose effect is shown, not charged. (5) Up to three currencies, in one “Currencies you trade in” table, each with its own rate; each shipment chooses one, and one “what if” setting per side moves every rate together (question 2 below). The accounting basis for all four is recorded in design/CALCULATION-ASSUMPTIONS.md §1 (NZ IAS 21, NZ IFRIC 22, NZ IAS 2).

Why it works this way

Each payment to a supplier is converted at the rate on the day it is paid, and money paid before the goods arrive becomes part of what the stock cost. A rate move only matters for money not yet paid or collected, which is what the tiles above show.

Six questions, all ruled by Mike on 2026-09-26, one at a time

  1. ✅ RULED 2026-09-26: the rate is written “1 NZD buys 0.6000 USD”, the way NZ banks quote it, not “1 USD costs 1.6667 NZD”. The word “buys” states the direction, so a rate copied from a supplier's quote the other way round reads as wrong.
  2. ✅ RULED 2026-09-26: up to three currencies, in one “Currencies you trade in” table, each with its own rate — not one supplier and one customer currency for the whole forecast. Each shipment chooses one; the typed stock grid and the sales grid each choose one; one “what if” setting per side moves every rate together. Mike asked whether three was easier now than later. Forecasts saved before it default to one currency, so it was never a trap — but the screen was being redrawn anyway.
  3. ✅ RULED 2026-09-26: the “Exchange-rate movement” line is hidden on the profit and loss and the cash flow whenever it is zero, and appears by itself when it has a figure — not shown at zero on every forecast. With one rate per currency it is zero today, so the rate risk lives in the what-if tiles. IAS 1.29-31 (present what is material); FRS-42 paras 41, 51, 55 and 58 (same policies as historical accounts, disclose significant assumptions and the effect of uncertainty). Refines the 2026-09-04 ruling rather than reversing it: when there is a movement it is still its own line.
  4. ✅ RULED 2026-09-26: both what-if settings start at 10% on a new forecast, the same figure the allowances use today; the advisor changes it per forecast.
  5. ✅ RULED 2026-09-26: the opening “Still owed to the supplier on landing” gets a currency picker from the “Currencies you trade in” table; it converts at that rate and is included in the what-if tile — not left as an NZD amount outside it. The deposits stay in NZD, because they are already paid. The drawing first recommended leaving it in NZD; the three-currency table made the picker small, and the recommendation changed before Mike ruled.
  6. ✅ RULED 2026-09-26: the wording below is approved by Mike exactly as written.
