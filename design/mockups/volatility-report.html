COACH What to say about this

Two-thirds of the months land inside the normal range, which is what the model predicts. Going up and down is normal. An owner who reacts to every month above or below the average is permanently passing or failing against a line that was never a target.

Four months sit outside the range and none is beyond the second deviation. The report shows where a month sits. Whether it is seasonal, a one-off, or something the business did to itself is a judgement for you and the client — not for the arithmetic.

Two upload states that must be seen, because both produce a believable wrong answer if they are not. The first is the join: two exports that do not meet leave a hole in the middle of the series, and a gap read as continuous months is a volatility figure that is simply false.

The two files do not meet. This year’s export starts in September; last year’s ends in March. Five months are missing between them, so a 24-month window cannot be built from these two files. Export the missing year, or measure 12 months instead.
⚠
The two files overlap. Both exports cover January to March. Last year’s figures were used for those three months and this year’s ignored. If that is the wrong way round, replace the older file.

  3. “Accounts” as the heading over the two files. Alternatives: “Your accounts”, “Accounts files”, “Where the figures come from”.
  4. “This year” and “Last year” for the two slots. Alternatives: “Most recent year” / “The year before”. Note these are financial years, not calendar ones — worth saying so on screen, or not?
  5. “Set aside” for a month left out of the maths. Alternatives: “not counted”, “left out”, “ignored”. This one matters most — it is the sentence that stops a believable wrong answer, so it has to be read and understood, not skimmed.
  6. “Part-finished” for the mid-month export. Alternatives: “incomplete”, “still in progress”, “not a full month”.

Three deliberate departures from the approved original (the pre-upload mockup at ddeffcf), named here rather than discovered later. (1) The original showed one source file; this shows two — Mike ruled on 2026-08-31 for “two files, up to 24 months”, because a Xero by-month export covers a single financial year and the 18 and 24-month windows need more than one. (2) Four step chips became five. (3) The original tagged only the result tiles “from file”; the month boxes are now tagged too, since that is where a figure can be seen and corrected.

And four departures of the BUILD from this page, which is the comparison the rule actually asks for. (a) The step chips appear only once an accounts file is chosen. This page draws them unconditionally, but on the typed path steps 1 and 2 can never complete, and a permanently unfinished checklist would tell an advisor who typed their figures that they had not finished. (b) “Replace” sits on each file row rather than as one “Replace accounts files” button in the actions row — with two slots the button has to say which file. (c) The set-aside months carry a heading, “Months set aside”; this page shows them as two amber boxes with no label, which reads as part of the series above them. (d) The “from file” tag is conditional. On Total sales it appears only when EVERY measured month came from the accounts, and on the highest/lowest tiles only when that particular month did — this page shows all three unconditionally. One overtyped figure and the tag would otherwise credit the accounts for a hand-keyed number.

Why the set-aside notice exists at all. design/REPORT-DATA-MODEL.md §3.9 records a real client export checked on 2026-07-15: months after the data cut-off read as a genuine 0, and the last populated month is usually partial. Fed straight into this model, those months lower the average and widen the standard deviation, producing a volatility score that is wrong and entirely plausible. The screen is where a person catches that, which is why the months are shown rather than silently consumed.
