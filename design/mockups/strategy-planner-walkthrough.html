0

Opening the Planner Built

Before the client arrives. About twenty seconds.

What the advisor does

  1. Opens Strategy Planner from the navigation.
  2. The page opens headed “What do you want from your session?” — your own slide title.
  3. Picks the client from one dropdown: “Which client?”
  4. Presses “Start the session”. Nothing can start without a client, because a plan belongs to somebody.

What is already on the screen

The subtitle, permanently: “Your role is to challenge what the client believes to be true. Their role is to explore with an open heart and an open mind.”

That is your own page 2 wording, and it is the only part of it the app carries today.

One panel per deck, in your order. Your five columns: Page · Framework · Concept Summary · Helps Your Client To… · Include. Fifty-two concepts across the four Planning Domains.

What the advisor does

  1. Reads what the AI already ticked. It pre-ticks with a reason against each row and never unticks anything — the advisor is always the one who removes.
  2. Adjusts the ticks. Ticks cross decks freely: Pivot takes nine concepts from Strategic Orientation 2 and two from Sales & Marketing Review.
  3. Watches the counter: “14 of 52 included”.

What the client sees

Often this table itself, on the screen between them. The words are yours, read off the decks — the app never rewrites them.

☑ One line on this screen was out of date, and was corrected the same day

At the foot of the scope screen the app used to say: “The screens that teach each concept and capture the client's answers are still being built — nothing typed is kept yet.” It had not been true since stage 2 shipped — those screens exist and every box is saved against the client's session — so an advisor was being told their work would be lost when it would not. Found by walking this journey on 2026-09-20 and fixed the same session on Mike's ruling. It now says what comes next and that the work is kept.

What the advisor does

  1. Types a step name. Identify the Resistance. Free text — nothing is suggested and the AI writes none of it.
  2. Drags five concepts into it from the list on the left.
  3. Repeats: Choose Your Competition Fronts (six), Rehearse the Message (two), Trial the Prototype (one).
  4. Adds a fifth step, names it Do It & Review It, and leaves it empty — in your deck that step is a promise to the client, not a framework.
Porter's is one concept and two cards. Observations go in step 1, responses in step 2. Both write into the same table. The two cards already exist in the app; this screen only decides where each one goes.

Nothing is lost by getting it wrong

A concept dragged out of a step goes back to the left-hand list, and anything already typed into its table is untouched — the answers are stored against the concept, never against the step.

🎙 Record Rivalry is fierce — four local firms…

Your fill-in table on the right, read from your own workbook — every label is yours, none authored by us.

What the advisor does

  1. Teaches the concept from the left of the screen.
  2. Captures what the client says on the right — typed, or spoken into the voice bar that sits on every box, the same control used everywhere else in the app.
  3. Moves to the next concept and repeats.

This is where the step builder changes the day

Today the fourteen cards come in whatever order the data produces, so Porter's observations and Porter's responses sit next to each other. With steps, the cards are grouped under your names and run in your order — Porter's responses come an hour later, in step 2, after the client has seen four more concepts. That gap is why the responses are worth more than the observations.

Everything is saved as it is typed

Box by box, against this client's session. If a save fails the message says so and keeps the words on screen — an advisor mid-meeting is never silently losing a box. The app also records which box was open when words were spoken, so a recording can be apportioned later without any AI deciding where anything belongs.

4

Objectives & actions — closing the session Built

The last half hour, still in the room.

What the advisor does

  1. Writes the Strategic Objective with the client, and the Strategy in the deck's four-box language.
  2. Fills in the Action Plan as a table — the task, who owns it, when.
  3. Picks a Growth Aspect for each action from a list.

The coverage check

The nine Growth Aspects wheel fills in, in your own colours, showing what the session covered and what it missed — Strategic Orientation 1's own instruction that objectives be tested against the nine.

No model reads an objective and guesses its aspect. The advisor picks it. A guess would be invisible when wrong.
5

The plan — what the client walks away with Built

Assembled instantly from everything above. The advisor types nothing here.

One continuous document of slide-shaped pages. It scrolls as one document on screen and is your deck, page for page, when printed or put on a projector. It stores nothing of its own, so it can never disagree with the session behind it. An untouched table prints one line — “Scoped for this session, not worked through yet” — rather than two dozen blank rows.

Two things are wrong with the left-hand column, and both are on pages a client reads

  • The agenda names our software. “Run the frameworks” and “Objectives and actions” are the labels on our own wizard. The app has one step, no name of its own to give it, so it borrowed ours and printed them on your client's plan.
  • The session has no shape. Fourteen concepts taught in one unbroken run, then fourteen tables worked in one unbroken run. Your Pivot teaches five and works five, then teaches six and works six.

⚠ And one page is missing from both columns

Your page 2 — “Our Session Objective”, the welcome, roles and housekeeping — is in six of your decks and in none of the app. It is drawn separately and is not part of the step builder.
