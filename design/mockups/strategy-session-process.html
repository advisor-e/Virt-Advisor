Four things to notice

  • The advisor starts with a process, not a blank page. The badge says who set it — set by the mentor — and Start from blank instead is one click away, so the standard is a starting point and never a cage.
  • The tray is grouped by deck with a count on each, so the left column reads as structure instead of forty identical boxes.
  • A suggestion sits where it would land — inside the step it belongs to, carrying the reason in plain English, with Accept and Not this session. It is dashed and tinted so nothing suggested can be mistaken for something chosen.
  • The conversation is a box on this screen, not a separate place to go. What comes back arrives as more suggestions in the steps, never as prose to read and act on.

Approved, and built — apart from the AI suggestions

Mike approved this drawing on 2026-09-21, having read it, and then said yes to building it as its own separate question. Decision B's suggestion panel is NOT built and is the one difference: where a suggestion comes from is stage 6 and unwritten, which this page says below under What this drawing does NOT decide. Decision A — does the standard process arrive already filled, or only named? — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-21. A handed-down step arrives with its concepts already placed inside it. The advisor drags out anything he does not want for this client; the scope he ticked on Scope session is never changed by it, and a concept dragged out returns to the tray with whatever has been typed into its table untouched.

Recommendation kept, so a later session can see what was proposed as well as what was decided: name and concepts both, because a named-but-empty step still leaves all 42 placements to do by hand, which is the cost being removed.

Asked as: when the mentor's standard session loads into Build session, should each step arrive with its concepts already placed inside it — instead of arriving as a name only with an empty drop zone to fill?

Decision B — what the AI is allowed to do here — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-21. The AI proposes and nothing else. It offers a concept into the step it would belong to with one line saying why; it never places, never removes and never reorders. Nothing moves until the advisor presses Accept. 🔴 Three consequences that bind the build: (a) a declined suggestion is recorded as offered and refused, so the same one does not return next session; (b) the suggestion and what the advisor did with it are both stored — the Original / AI Suggestion / Final Approved Value trail the engineering standards already require; (c) the reason line is model output and is validated before it is shown, so a suggestion naming a concept that is not in scope is dropped rather than drawn.

Recommendation kept: it proposes into a step and writes one line of reason; it never places, never removes, and never reorders. This is the Build session twin of the scope menu's "The AI pre-ticks, never unticks" — the AI proposes, the human decides.

Asked as: should the AI only ever suggest a concept into a step, with nothing moving until the advisor presses Accept — instead of placing concepts itself and leaving him to remove the ones he does not want?

Decision C — which tiers may hold their own process — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-21. All four manager tiers may write their own standard session — mentor, global group manager, group manager, firm manager. The advisor edits only the session in front of him, and his changes never become the firm's standard by accident. 🔴 This is a stated judgement, not the default: the standing rule is the mentor tier alone unless a lower tier genuinely needs to differ, and a firm's planning method is exactly what one firm does differently from another. A tier that has written nothing shows what it inherits and says whose it is, so an inherited process is never mistaken for an authored one.

Recommendation kept: all four managing tiers author, the advisor edits per session only. The ladder is the one every cascading block already uses — parentScopeOf in server/utils/tierChain.js — so overriding brings version history and restore with it, exactly as the Advisory Distinctions table already does.

Asked as: should all four manager tiers each be able to write their own version of the standard session — instead of only the mentor writing one, with every firm receiving that single process?

Decision D — the two closing blocks — RULED

Ruled AS RECOMMENDED — Mike, 2026-09-21. Strategic Objective and Strategy and Operational Objectives and Tactics leave Build session entirely and appear only on Objectives & actions, where they already live. They are the app's own closing blocks, not concepts off any of Mike's decks — which is why they were the only two cards in the tray with no deck beneath them. ⚠ This reverses part of Decision 4 of 2026-09-20, which made them ordinary cards on an ordinary step so their names would stop reaching the client's agenda. That reason stands and is satisfied better this way: out of the step builder, they name nothing on the agenda at all.

Recommendation kept: take them out of this screen. They are the closing half of every session, they already have their own stage, and asking an advisor to sort them alongside concepts off the decks is what made the tray read as arbitrary.

Asked as: should the two closing blocks be taken out of the Build session list so they appear only on Objectives & actions — instead of staying in the list as cards to file into a step?

What this drawing does NOT decide

  • Where the AI's suggestions come from. The concept index built 2026-09-17 makes them possible — all 11 of Pivot's concepts resolve to their own record — but the selection itself is stage 6 and unwritten. This screen draws where its answers land.
  • Wording. Every label here is a placeholder until Mike gives the words.
  • The mentor's authoring screen in full. Screen 2 shows the shape and the ladder; the tab, its place in the hub, and version history follow the pattern already built, and are drawn properly once Decision C is answered.
